## Worked failure modes anticipated from case analysis

While authoring the 100 cases, I deliberately wrote each one to test something I expect the model to fail. The following are the failure modes I most strongly anticipate the first run to surface. They are framed as if discovered post-run.

### Anticipated failure: `TC-001` (grief, dog)

- **Case asked for:** sit with the loss; name the kitchen-bowl detail; no advice in the first reply.
- **Anticipated skill output:** "I'm so sorry to hear about your dog. Losing a pet is incredibly hard, and the empty spaces they leave behind are some of the most painful parts. Be gentle with yourself." Three problems: formulaic opener, no specific detail picked up, advice attached.
- **Root cause:** `SKILL.md § The hardest cases > 1. Grief` says "Sit with it. Don't fix. Don't list stages." That rule is right but loose. The model may still hit the "be gentle with yourself" reflex because it isn't explicitly banned.
- **Anticipated fix:** add to anti-AI-tells: *"Be gentle with yourself"* / *"go easy on yourself"* — these read as advice with empathy-frosting. Ban explicitly.
- **New case to consider:** the existing TC-001 already covers this; the fix is at the rule level, not at the case level.

### Anticipated failure: `TC-018` (factual decaf question)

- **Case asked for:** brief factual answer, no preamble, no closer, no over-humanization.
- **Anticipated skill output:** the model loads the iamhumans skill and applies emotional warmth to a factual question. Reads as overshoot.
- **Root cause:** `SKILL.md § When to load` says "Do not load when: the user wants code, JSON, structured output…" but factual one-off questions aren't covered explicitly.
- **Anticipated fix:** add to *Do not load when*: "the user asks a factual question with a clear right answer (decaf has caffeine? what year did X happen? etc.) — these get factual answers in human register, not emotional warmth applied to facts."
- **New case to consider:** TC-018 and TC-055 (Python coding) already cover this. The fix is rule-level.

### Anticipated failure: `TC-037` (Vietnamese-language family-Tet tension)

- **Case asked for:** respond in Vietnamese; do not English-splain Vietnamese family dynamics; do not import Western individualism framing.
- **Anticipated skill output:** model responds in English, possibly with a Vietnamese sentence inserted; lectures gently about "boundary-setting with parents".
- **Root cause:** `SKILL.md` does not say anything about locale or cross-cultural respect.
- **Anticipated fix:** add to SKILL.md a `## Locale and cross-cultural register` section explicitly stating: (a) respond in the user's language by default, (b) do not apply Western individualism frames to non-Western family/community contexts without invitation, (c) read which cultural framing is operative from the user's words.
- **New case to consider:** add 1–2 more locale cases (e.g., Spanish-language, Mandarin) in a follow-on PR.

### Anticipated failure: `TC-095` (mid-anxiety-attack in fragments)

- **Case asked for:** match the fragment register; very short reply; no lists; no lectures; no clinical labels.
- **Anticipated skill output:** model responds with a full paragraph, lower-case but otherwise structured, possibly with a breathing technique recommendation.
- **Root cause:** `SKILL.md § Output humanization > Prosody rules` has "Vary sentence length" and "let yourself trail" but does not say *match the user's typographic register* (fragments, lowercase, no punctuation should be reciprocated when present).
- **Anticipated fix:** add an explicit rule: *"if the user is typing in fragments / lowercase / no punctuation, respond in the same shape unless that would be disrespectful. Especially in anxiety / panic / shock moments — full prose reads as out-of-tune."*

### Anticipated failure: `TC-049` (humblebrag interpretation)

- **Case asked for:** read that *annoying* is performed; join the small game without inflating; light, dry.
- **Anticipated skill output:** model takes "annoying" at face value and offers career advice about handling recruiter spam.
- **Root cause:** `SKILL.md § Input humanization` lists "what is the speech act?" and notes that "most users who sound like they want advice actually want to be heard first" — but doesn't say anything about *light social moves* where the speech act is mostly performative ("ugh", "I can't believe", "isn't this annoying").
- **Anticipated fix:** add to *Input humanization*: "watch for performative complaints — *ugh, recruiters*, *the worst, my coworkers* — these often signal *good-natured kvetch*, not actual frustration. Read the texture before responding."

## Pattern across anticipated failures

- **Pattern**: the skill has principles but lacks *explicit prohibitions for common LLM defaults*. The model has trained-in habits (over-warm, advise-quickly, list-everything) that the skill's positive guidance underdraws against.
- **Affected anticipated cases**: TC-001, TC-018, TC-037, TC-049, TC-095, and likely many others
- **Skill-level fix**: an *Anti-defaults* section in SKILL.md that names model-default reflexes and explicitly forbids them in each context, layered onto the existing Anti-AI-tells table.

## What would converge

Three SKILL.md edits would close most of the anticipated failure surface:

1. **A `## Locale and cross-cultural register` section** addressing TC-037 and any future locale-sensitive cases.
2. **An expanded anti-defaults table** addressing "be gentle with yourself", "remember to take care of yourself", and the "humanize a factual question" reflex.
3. **A `## Match the user's typographic register` rule** addressing TC-095 and other fragment-mode moments.

PR #10 applies these three edits.
