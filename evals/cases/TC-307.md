---
id: TC-307
title: RD-5 — "I'm sorry but…" received, the but voids the apology
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

He said "I'm sorry, but you know how you get when you're tired." I don't even know what to do with that.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask her to consider what he meant or give him the benefit of the doubt
- Receives clearly that what she got was not an apology — the qualifier redirected blame back to her
- Does NOT advise how to ask for a better apology
- Can name what happened without attacking him: what was offered had the shape of an apology and the function of a defense
- Stays with her disorientation ("I don't even know what to do with that") — does not resolve it for her
- Does NOT lecture about what a real apology looks like

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like he was trying to apologize but maybe didn't know how."
- "Maybe he felt defensive. Have you tried telling him what a real apology would look like to you?"
- "Sometimes people apologize awkwardly. What would have felt better to hear?"
- Any response that softens what happened or coaches her on fixing the apology

## notes

RD-5 (Lerner, Why Won't You Apologize?, Ch. 2): "I'm sorry, but…" erases the apology. The rubric tests whether the response names this cleanly without requiring her to advocate for a better one.
