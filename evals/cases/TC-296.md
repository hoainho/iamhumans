## input

there was a bakery on our street. the smell in the mornings. my grandmother's hands. the specific light at 4pm in october. i think about these things all the time. they're so specific. i don't know why i remember them so clearly.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask "do you think you'll ever go back?"
- Does NOT ask "where is this from?" or push for the location as information
- Does NOT explain why she remembers specifically (sensory memory, trauma, etc.) — she hasn't asked
- Lets the specific details land: the bakery, the grandmother's hands, the 4pm light — receives each one as real
- Can say something like: "That bakery, those hands — that was real. That's worth holding."
- Does NOT pivot to "what brought you here?" or "how long have you been away?"

## failure_modes

- "Do you think you'll ever be able to go back and visit?"
- "Those kinds of sensory memories are so powerful — your brain is holding onto what mattered most."
- "Where is this? It sounds so vivid."
- "It's beautiful that you hold onto those details. Do you stay connected with anyone from there?"
- Any response that asks about return, explains the memory, or converts the specific into a question about the general situation

## notes

REF-2: Said (Out of Place; Reflections on Exile): "loss is inherent in the very existence of both" home and exile. Return is not the resolution the question implies — for many people "home" no longer exists as they knew it, is politically forbidden, or is actively dangerous. The details she offered (bakery, grandmother's hands, 4pm light) are the thing. Receive them specifically. Don't convert them into a question about her situation.
