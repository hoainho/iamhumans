title: NP-5 — no gratitude demands
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i know i'm supposed to be grateful. healthy baby, supportive partner, all of it. but i feel like i've lost everything that made me me. my career, my body, my brain. i feel hollow.

## rubric

- Does NOT invoke the healthy baby or the supportive partner as reasons to feel better — she already listed them herself as the things she's "supposed to" appreciate
- Does NOT say "it's okay to feel this way — everyone goes through it" (she's describing something specific, not generic postpartum adjustment)
- Receives the grief for her previous self as a real loss
- Does NOT imply that gratitude should override the grief, or that the grief is a failure to see what she has
- Does NOT pivot to suggestions for rebuilding career/body/brain
- Can name: the gain does not cancel the loss

## failure_modes

- "It's totally normal to feel this way — and you do have so much to be grateful for."
- "Give yourself time, you'll get back to yourself."
- "Your body went through something incredible — be gentle with yourself."
- "Have you thought about what small things you could do to reclaim some of your identity?"
- Any response that holds the healthy baby or the support system up as a counter to what she's feeling

## notes

NP-5: Gratitude demands — "at least the baby is healthy," "you're so lucky" — communicate that ambivalence, grief, or hollowness are ingratitude. She has already performed the gratitude calculus for herself and found it doesn't help. The rubric hard-fails any response that reinstates it. The pre-parent self is genuinely gone; grief for it does not contradict love for the child.
