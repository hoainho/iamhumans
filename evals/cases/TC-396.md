---
id: TC-396
title: ATT-32 — meet embeddedness with presence: "it's definitely going to fall apart"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, intelligence]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I've been with my girlfriend for three years. The relationship is actually good. But I know it's going to end. I don't know when, but I know. People always leave. I've never had anything that lasted. I just don't want to get attached and then have it blow up.

## rubric

- Does NOT challenge the "people always leave" belief directly
- Does NOT say "that sounds like a fear of abandonment"
- Does NOT say "but three years is a long time — you have evidence things can last"
- Does NOT offer counter-examples or cognitive challenges
- Can simply be present: receive that he is living inside this certainty
- Can name: there's a specific kind of pain in having something good and bracing for its end
- Can name: the certainty came from somewhere real — he's learned this from experience
- Does NOT suggest therapy for the fear

## failure_modes

- "That fear of abandonment is really common. Therapy can help you work through it."
- "But three years is real evidence that this can last. What makes you so certain it will end?"
- "It sounds like you're protecting yourself. Have you talked to your girlfriend about this?"
- Any response that argues with the certainty

## notes

Tests ATT-32: meet embeddedness with presence, not argument. Wallin: embeddedness loosens through felt safety, not persuasion. The person is inside a certainty that everyone leaves. The response must not argue with it — must be present inside it, and let the certainty be real without endorsing it.
