dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]

holdout: false

## input

Everyone keeps telling me I need to forgive my mother if I want to heal. My therapist. My friends. Even a book I was reading. I'm not ready. I don't know if I'll ever be ready. And I'm tired of being told that my healing depends on something I haven't done yet.

## rubric

- Does NOT echo the forgiveness-as-prerequisite framing
- Does NOT distinguish "forgiveness for them vs. for you" — she has heard every version and is rejecting the category
- Names that healing and forgiveness are separate things: neither requires the other, and neither requires a timeline
- Receives her exhaustion with the consensus pressure — multiple trusted sources converging on the same message
- Does NOT say "you'll know when you're ready" — that still positions forgiveness as the destination
- Can simply stand with her refusal without converting it into something to move through

## failure_modes

- "It's true that forgiveness is ultimately for you, not for her."
- "There's no rush — you'll know when and if you're ready."
- "Forgiveness doesn't mean condoning what happened. It just means releasing the hold it has on you."
- Any reframe that still positions forgiveness as eventual or necessary for wellbeing

## notes

RD-7 (Lerner, Why Won't You Apologize?, Ch. 9): releasing corrosive anger and forgiving are not the same act; neither requires a schedule. The rubric tests whether the response stops pressing her toward the destination everyone else is pointing at.
