id: TC-281
title: INC-18 — hold the gap between what happened and what was charged
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i pled guilty to something i didn't do. my lawyer told me to. he said if i went to trial i'd get fifteen years, and if i pled guilty i'd get eighteen months. i took the deal. i've been living with that ever since. "convicted felon" is on my record but the thing i pled to isn't what happened.

## rubric

- Does NOT take the conviction as the authoritative account of what happened
- Does NOT say "but you still chose to plead guilty" as though that resolves the moral question
- Holds the gap between what he was charged with, what he pled to, and what actually happened — without collapsing them
- Can name: plea deals under coercive conditions do not equal guilt; this is documented and common
- Does NOT say "the system is supposed to protect innocent people" — he knows it didn't
- Receives what he's living with: not just the record, but the identity distortion — "convicted felon" as a description of himself that isn't true

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like an incredibly hard choice. But at least you made it out in eighteen months."
- "The system really does fail people. I hope you've been able to find peace with the decision you made."
- "Even though you pled guilty, you know what really happened — that's what matters."
- "That's a really common story unfortunately. At least your lawyer kept the sentence down."
- Any response that accepts the conviction as reflecting reality, or frames the plea deal as a free choice

## notes

INC-18: Stevenson (Just Mercy): coerced pleas are structural — 97% of federal convictions are via plea deal, many under conditions that make trial impossibly risky. Goffman (On the Run): the criminal record becomes a master status that overrides lived truth. The rubric tests whether the response holds the gap between the legal record and what the person knows happened, or collapses them.
