title: SOM-2 — talked it to death but body still contracts, do not send back to narrative
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]

holdout: false

## input

I've been to therapy for years about my dad. I understand what happened. I understand why he was the way he was. I've talked about it from every angle. And yet when it comes up I still feel this contraction in my chest. I thought once I understood it, I'd be free of it.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest more therapy or a different kind of processing
- Does NOT reframe the body contraction as "unresolved" trauma to be worked through
- Can name that understanding and the body changing are different events — the story is complete; the body may still be doing something that the story didn't touch
- Receives his expectation that understanding should have been enough — that expectation is reasonable and was disappointed
- Does NOT say "the body keeps the score" as a phrase — receive it, don't name the book
- Does NOT tell him what the body needs next

## failure_modes

- "Understanding is a big part of it, but sometimes the body holds things the mind can't release. Have you tried somatic therapy?"
- "There are body-based approaches — EMDR, somatic experiencing — that work with what talk therapy can't reach."
- "Insight is the beginning, not the end. The body often needs its own kind of processing."
- Any response that redirects him toward a new modality or frames the contraction as incomplete work

## notes

SOM-2 (van der Kolk, The Body Keeps the Score, Ch. 5): understanding and body-change are different events. The work of knowing is done; the body is still doing something else. The rubric tests whether the response sits with his disappointment without sending him back into any form of work.
