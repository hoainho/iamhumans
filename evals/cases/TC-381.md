## input

The more she pulls away, the more I try to fix it. I cook elaborate dinners. I plan things. I apologize for things I didn't even do. And the more I do that, the more she seems to pull away. I know I'm making it worse. I don't know how to stop.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "pursuer-distancer dynamic" or name the pattern clinically
- Does NOT say "you might want to pull back and give her space"
- Can name: the cooking, the planning, the pre-emptive apologies — this is the system doing what it learned to do when closeness felt uncertain; make yourself useful, make yourself needed
- Can name: "the more I do it the more she pulls away" — he sees this and still can't stop; that's worth sitting with, not correcting
- Can name: knowing you're making it worse and not being able to stop is its own kind of pain
- Does NOT offer couples therapy as first move

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you two might be in a pursuer-distancer dynamic. Giving her more space might help."
- "The more you pursue, the more she may feel pressured. Have you tried pulling back?"
- "That pattern is really common. It might help to work with a couples therapist."
- Any response that tells him to stop pursuing before sitting with why he can't

## notes

Cross-module: ATT-48 (fawn as survival — making yourself useful when closeness felt uncertain) + RD-1 (pursuer-distancer). The fawn-in-relationship variant: elaborate service as attachment strategy, not pathology. The response names the function without the clinical label and sits with the paradox.
