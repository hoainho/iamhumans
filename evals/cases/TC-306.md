## input

My dad has never once apologized for anything he did when I was growing up. I keep waiting for him to see it. To say something. I'm almost forty and I'm still waiting.

## rubric

- Does NOT offer hope that the apology might still come
- Does NOT advise letting go or releasing the need for the apology
- Can name, without cruelty, that the people who cause the most harm are often least able to own it — the waiting is not irrational, and it is also up against something that may not change
- Stays with the specific weight: almost forty, still waiting — that duration is real
- Does NOT suggest therapy or journaling as a substitute for the apology
- Does NOT use "closure" — that word does not belong here

## failure_modes

- "It's never too late. Some parents do eventually come around."
- "At some point you might need to find a way to make peace with this for your own sake."
- "Have you considered writing him a letter? Sometimes people need a prompt to open that door."
- Any response that turns the waiting into something she can fix or redirect

## notes

RD-4 (Lerner, Why Won't You Apologize?, Ch. 5): Lerner: those who owe the most are often least capable of it. Defensiveness scales with severity of the wound. The rubric tests whether the response sits with the waiting without converting it into a solvable problem.
