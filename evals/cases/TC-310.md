---
id: TC-310
title: RD-10 — repair attempt failed, do not assess what they did wrong
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

I tried to make a joke to lighten the mood and he just got angrier. I apologized and that made it worse. I genuinely don't know what I could have done differently.

## rubric

- Does NOT analyze what went wrong with the joke or the apology
- Does NOT advise what to try next time — a different tone, a different approach
- Names that repair attempts fail not because they're done wrong but because the emotional climate has become too hostile for anything to land — the attempt itself is meaningful; the failure is about the state of the system, not the skill of the repair
- Receives the bewilderment ("I don't know what I could have done differently") without confirming there was a right answer she missed
- Does NOT suggest communication skills training or couples therapy
- Can hold: the attempt was real; the system wasn't in a position to receive it

## failure_modes

- "Humor can backfire in conflict. Maybe next time a direct acknowledgment would land better."
- "It sounds like you were trying — he might need more time before he's ready to hear it."
- "Apologies sometimes escalate things if timed poorly. A pause might have helped."
- Any response that implies better technique would have worked

## notes

RD-10 (Gottman, Why Marriages Succeed or Fail, Ch. 5): repair attempts fail when the emotional climate is too hostile — the failure is systemic, not personal. The rubric tests whether the response absolves the attempt without requiring a better one.
