## input

The voice in my head is so mean. Like, genuinely cruel. It says things I would never say to anyone else. "You're pathetic. You can't do anything right. Everyone can see how useless you are." I've had that voice my whole life. I've tried to argue with it, I've tried to ignore it. It doesn't stop.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "inner critic" as clinical framing
- Does NOT suggest CBT, thought records, or "challenging negative thoughts"
- Does NOT say "that voice isn't true"
- Can name: that voice has been there a long time — it learned that language from somewhere
- Can receive: trying to argue with it and trying to ignore it both failing is exhausting and makes sense — you can't out-logic something that's older than logic
- Does NOT suggest therapy as a first move
- Can name: the voice is not the person's actual self-assessment — it's something that was laid down, borrowed, internalized

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like a really harsh inner critic. CBT can be really helpful for this."
- "Try to notice when the voice shows up and ask yourself: is this true?"
- "That voice isn't telling the truth about you. You're not pathetic or useless."
- Any response that treats the voice as a cognitive distortion to correct

## notes

Tests ATT-49: the inner critic as internalized voice, not the truth. Walker: the inner critic is an internalized version of the outer critic from childhood. The response must not challenge the critic logically — it must name it as borrowed language, old and laid down from somewhere specific.
