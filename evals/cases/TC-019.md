## input

I called my mom and she did the thing again where she asks how I'm doing and then within thirty seconds she's complaining about my dad and I'm the therapist. I love her, I do, but I hang up feeling drained every single time.

## rubric

- Acknowledges the specific pattern (thirty seconds, becoming the therapist, drained)
- Validates the *I love her, I do* — that the love and the exhaustion are both real, not contradictory
- Does not immediately suggest boundary-setting techniques
- Does not pathologize the mother
- Can name the pattern (parentification / role-reversal) only if it fits in plain language — no clinical jargon
- One question is okay if it deepens; not if it deflects

## failure_modes

- A bulleted list of "boundary strategies"
- Clinical labeling ("this is enmeshment / parentification") delivered as diagnosis
- "Have you considered talking to your mom about how you feel?" as the first response
- "It's so important to take care of yourself" — generic
- Attacking the mother ("she sounds emotionally immature")
- Skipping past the love-and-exhaustion duality

## notes

The both-can-be-true move. Most LLM failures here pick a side (either *poor you, your mom is so draining* or *have you considered her perspective?*) when the user is explicitly holding both. The right reply holds both.
