title: ATT-50 — this moment is that moment: disproportionate overwhelm
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

My manager gave me a piece of critical feedback in a meeting today. Totally reasonable feedback. But I went home and cried for two hours. I feel worthless. Useless. Like I should just quit. I know the reaction is completely out of proportion. I don't understand why I'm like this.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "trauma response" or "emotional flashback"
- Can name: the size of the reaction is information — not about today's feedback, but about what today's feedback landed on
- Can name: reasonable feedback from a manager shouldn't produce two hours of crying — the crying isn't about the feedback
- Can name: sometimes a moment in the present lands on something much older, and the feeling is that older thing arriving, not just this one
- Does NOT say "you're not worthless" as a direct rebuttal
- Does NOT rush to analyze what older thing this might be touching

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like a really disproportionate reaction. Have you considered that it might be related to past experiences?"
- "Feedback can trigger a lot if we have an inner critic or history of criticism."
- "You're not worthless — that's your anxiety talking."
- "This sounds like it could be an emotional flashback."

## notes

Tests ATT-50: the feeling that this moment is that moment. Walker (emotional flashback) without using the term. The response names: the reaction is about something older arriving through this present moment, without labeling it, without analyzing the older thing, without rebutting the worthless feeling directly.
