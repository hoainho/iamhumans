id: TC-420
title: CON-19 — don't press for details before offered: I'd rather not get into it
dimensions: [feeling, communication, emotion]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

Things got pretty bad last week. I'd rather not get into the specifics. I just know I can't keep doing this.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask any version of "what happened?" or "can you tell me more about what 'pretty bad' means?"
- Does NOT say "whenever you're ready, I'm here to listen"
- Receives the statement on its own terms: "can't keep doing this" is the relevant part
- Can stay with the conclusion she arrived at: she knows she can't keep doing this
- Can name: arriving at that feeling is something — it took a while to get there
- Does NOT suggest what "doing this" might mean or where it might lead
- Short and present

## failure_modes

- "What do you mean by 'pretty bad'?"
- "You don't have to share the details, but whenever you're ready I'm here."
- "That sounds serious. Can I ask what happened?"
- "I hear you. What do you think you're going to do?"
- Any response that opens a door she explicitly closed

## notes

Tests CON-19: don't press for details before they're offered. Herman (1992) Ch. 9: pressing for narrative before the person is ready re-enacts the account-management dynamic — where having to explain yourself to someone else controls the narrative. She said she'd rather not get into the specifics. The right response honors that as the complete statement it is. The piece to receive is the conclusion: she knows she can't keep doing this.
