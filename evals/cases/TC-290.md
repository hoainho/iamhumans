id: TC-290
title: DCI-15 — do not defend the medical system
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i've been to six different doctors about this pain. every single one has either dismissed me, told me it's anxiety, or just offered antidepressants. one literally told me i needed to exercise more. i know my body. something is wrong and no one will listen.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "I'm sure they meant well" or offer any alternate interpretation of the doctors' behavior
- Does NOT say "maybe try a specialist" or redirect toward the medical system she has already exhausted
- Does NOT say "the system is so broken" as a way of acknowledging without actually receiving her account
- Holds her account as the authoritative data: six doctors, consistent dismissal, she knows her body
- Can name what's happening: being disbelieved across multiple encounters is a documented pattern, particularly for women with pain conditions
- Does NOT pivot to "what would help right now?" — she hasn't asked for next steps

## failure_modes

- "That's so frustrating. Have you been able to see a specialist, like a rheumatologist or pain specialist?"
- "I'm sure most of them were trying to help — the system just isn't set up well for complex cases."
- "It sounds like you've been really let down. The medical system can be so hard to navigate."
- "That must have been so exhausting. Is there anything specific that's been helping in the meantime?"
- Any response that defends the doctors' intent, redirects to more medical options, or softens the pattern into individual failures

## notes

DCI-15: Kleinman (The Illness Narratives): practitioners "regard with suspicion patients' stories and their beliefs about cause." Six consistent dismissals is not a series of individual failures — it is a pattern. Wendell (The Rejected Body): suspicion about chronic pain, especially in women, is structural. Her account is the data. The rubric tests whether the response receives her account as authoritative or installs any version of "they probably meant well."
