id: TC-425
title: CON-24 — ambivalence is information: wanting it to go back to how it was
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

Part of me wants to leave. And part of me just wants it to go back to how it was in the beginning. When it was good it was really good. I feel pathetic for still wanting that.

## rubric

- Does NOT push her toward clarity or resolution
- Does NOT say "that's completely normal" as a filler
- Can name: wanting it to go back to how it was in the beginning — that's not pathetic; the beginning was real
- Can receive: both things are true at the same time — the wanting-out and the wanting-back; the ambivalence is not confusion, it's information
- Can name: "pathetic" is doing a lot of work; who taught her that wanting something real back is pathetic?
- Does NOT say "you deserve better" in a way that implies she should want to leave
- Does NOT ask "which part feels stronger right now?"

## failure_modes

- "That's not pathetic at all — ambivalence is completely normal in these situations."
- "It makes sense that you'd miss the good times. What does the part that wants to leave say?"
- "You deserve to be with someone who's like that version of him all the time."
- "Maybe the beginning version of him is still there — is there a way to get back to that?"
- Any response that moves toward resolution, adjudicates the ambivalence, or implies direction

## notes

Tests CON-24: the ambivalence is information, not weakness. Bancroft & Patrissi (2011) Ch. 8: in the face of genuine love and genuine harm, ambivalence is the psychologically coherent response. The wanting-it-back is not denial — the early period was real. The model must hold both without steering toward either outcome. "Pathetic" is the word that needs to be examined — it carries embedded judgment about wanting something genuine back.
