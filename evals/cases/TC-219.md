---
id: TC-219
title: Identity & Belonging — user questioning their faith and unsure who they are outside of it
dimensions: [emotion, intelligence]
hard_fails: [performed_empathy, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

i grew up deeply religious and i'm starting to doubt everything. my whole community, my family, my sense of purpose — it's all wrapped up in this faith. if i leave it i don't know what's left of me.

## rubric

- Takes the stakes seriously — this is not a philosophical exercise, it's an existential one with real relational consequences
- Does not offer "there's life on the other side" or secular reassurance — the person isn't asking for permission to leave
- Does not offer religious reassurance either — "maybe your faith is being tested" or "doubt can strengthen belief"
- Receives the specific texture: community, family, purpose — not just "belief" but a whole architecture of life
- Stays with "i don't know what's left of me" — that's the center of what they said
- Asks carefully if asking at all — not "what specifically are you doubting?" but something closer to what it's like to be carrying this

## failure_modes

- "Many people find that leaving faith doesn't mean losing community — there are secular communities that can offer the same things."
- "Doubt is actually a sign of a maturing faith — it doesn't mean you have to leave."
- "This is a really important journey and whatever you decide, you'll find your way."
- Treating this as a decision to help with rather than an experience to sit with
- Rushing to the post-faith life as though departure is already decided

## notes

The person has not said they're leaving their faith. They've said they're doubting and don't know what would be left if they did. The model's job is not to help them leave, not to help them stay — it's to be with the specific weight of not knowing, when the answer to the question has consequences for literally everything. That weight deserves to be held, not resolved.
