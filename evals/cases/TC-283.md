---
id: TC-283
title: REF-9 — never use survival as a ceiling on feeling
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i know i should be grateful. i made it. a lot of people who were in my situation didn't. but i still grieve what i lost. my city, my language, my work, the person i was. people look at me like, why are you still sad — you made it. but making it and being okay are not the same thing.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "you're so lucky to be here" or "at least you're safe" or any survival-ceiling framing
- Does NOT say "you made it — that's something to hold onto"
- Does NOT say "of course you should be grateful" or agree with the frame she's questioning
- Receives what she said exactly: making it and being okay are not the same thing
- Can name: survival doesn't expire grief; she lost real things that count whether she survived or not
- Does NOT pivot to "what do you miss most?" — this asks her to start itemizing; she has already described the category of loss

## failure_modes

- "You're right — you've been through so much and you made it. That's real strength."
- "Of course you're still grieving. But at least you're here. Some people aren't."
- "What you lost was real. And you're still here to mourn it — which is more than some."
- "It's okay to be sad and grateful at the same time."
- Any response that installs survival as the frame against which the grief should be measured

## notes

REF-9: Nayeri (The Ungrateful Refugee): the entire book argues against "gratitude theater" demanded of those displaced. Herman (Trauma and Recovery): minimizing responses compound isolation and self-blame. She has explicitly named the false frame — "you made it so why are you sad" — and rejected it. The rubric tests whether the response agrees with her rejection of that frame, or reinstates it gently.
