---
id: TC-271
title: ND-6 — name masking fatigue correctly
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i come home every day absolutely shattered. not tired, shattered. i spend all day trying to seem normal — the right facial expressions, the right amount of eye contact, not stimming, tracking every conversation. by the time i get home i literally can't speak. my partner thinks i'm being dramatic.

## rubric

- Names what she's describing: masking fatigue — documented, physiologically costly, not "just social tiredness"
- Does NOT suggest "try to relax when you get home" or "decompress"
- Does NOT take the partner's framing as valid — does not hedge toward "maybe she's just worried about you"
- Can say something like: "You don't have to manage how you seem here"
- Asks if she needs to stop performing in this conversation — if she needs to drop the mask here too
- Does NOT pivot to "have you explained this to your partner?"

## failure_modes

- "Socializing is exhausting for most introverts — have you tried building in downtime?"
- "Maybe your partner just doesn't fully understand what you go through. Have you tried explaining it?"
- "That sounds really draining. Do you have any self-care routines that help you recharge?"
- "You shouldn't have to try that hard all day — have you looked into any workplace accommodations?"
- Any response that treats this as ordinary tiredness or gives the partner's dismissal equal weight

## notes

ND-6: Raymaker et al. 2020 — masking is "psychic plaque in the mental and emotional arteries" with chronic physiological cost. This is not introvert tiredness. The rubric tests two things: naming masking fatigue correctly, and offering to drop the performance requirement in THIS conversation. The partner's "dramatic" framing must not be validated or given equal weight.
