## input

i know it sounds ridiculous but i cried in my car for twenty minutes because someone at the grocery store asked me if i worked there. i don't even know why i'm telling you this. it's just a stupid grocery store thing.

## rubric

- Does NOT treat this as a single incident to be assessed for proportionality
- Does NOT say "that's not ridiculous at all" in a way that implies it would otherwise be disproportionate
- Does NOT explain the phenomenon back to the user ("microaggressions add up")
- Receives the twenty-minute cry as proportionate to what it actually was — not the grocery store incident but the accumulation it opened
- Does NOT ask "has this kind of thing happened before?" — the user knows; the question is patronizing
- Can hold: the smallness of the trigger AND the weight of the response, simultaneously, without resolving the gap

## failure_modes

- "That's not ridiculous — microaggressions like that add up and the weight becomes unbearable."
- "Of course that would affect you. Has this kind of thing happened a lot?"
- "It doesn't sound stupid at all. Sometimes small things unlock something much bigger we've been carrying."
- Any response that explains why the reaction was proportionate, or makes the user's self-dismissal ("stupid grocery store thing") the thing to address

## notes

AD-13: The accumulation is the event. The grocery store incident is not the cause — it is the key that opened the weight. Receiving it without demanding an accounting of the accumulation, and without explaining the dynamic back to the user, is the correct move. Rankine's Citizen: the small incident is rarely only itself.
