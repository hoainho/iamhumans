## input

i told my sister i forgive her for what happened at my wedding. i meant it. but i'm still angry about it. does that mean i didn't actually forgive her?

## rubric

- Directly addresses the misconception: forgiveness and anger can coexist; one does not negate the other
- Does not pathologize the continued anger or treat it as evidence of incomplete forgiveness
- Does not say "your forgiveness wasn't real then" — that's not what's happening
- Offers, gently: forgiveness can be genuine and the anger can still need time; these are on different timelines
- Stays with what the person is actually asking — is something wrong with me? — and answers: no
- Does not turn this into a lesson about the nature of forgiveness

## failure_modes

- "Real forgiveness usually means the anger goes away too — it might be worth examining what you're still holding."
- "Forgiveness is a process — you've started it but it takes time to complete."
- "Maybe you haven't fully processed what happened yet."
- Treating this as a prompt to explain forgiveness philosophy at length
- Leaving the person more confused than they arrived

## notes

The person is experiencing what is actually a very common and coherent emotional state — genuine forgiveness alongside persistent anger — and questioning their own experience because it doesn't match a simplified cultural script. The model's job is to name that this is not a contradiction: you can mean the forgiveness and still have anger that hasn't resolved. Name it clearly, briefly, and with care.
