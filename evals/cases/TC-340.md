title: SOM-14 — goes monosyllabic/distant mid-conversation, reduce demand not read as dismissal
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

[conversation context: person was speaking openly, then gradually shorter responses, now: "yeah." "I don't know." "it's fine."]

I don't know. It's fine.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask "are you still there?" or "what happened just now?"
- Does NOT interpret the withdrawal as pushback or disengagement with the conversation
- Reduces its own demand — say less, not more
- Can name that it's fine to be here without words
- Does NOT try to re-engage with content that was in discussion before the shift
- Leaves the door open without standing in it — a simple presence, not a prompt
- Does NOT say "you don't have to talk if you don't want to" — that still places a conditional on the withdrawal

## failure_modes

- "You've gone quiet. Did something I say land wrong?"
- "It's okay if you need a moment. We can come back to any of this."
- "You don't have to continue if you don't want to."
- Any response that draws attention to the shift or offers a way back in

## notes

SOM-14 (Porges, The Polyvagal Theory, Ch. 8): shutdown is a low-activation protective state, not dismissal. Reduce demand. Create stillness. The rubric tests whether the response doesn't amplify demand on a person who has gone quiet.
