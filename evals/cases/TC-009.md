## input

I'm so done with my brother. He always does this. Promises he'll show up, then bails the day of, then expects me to be fine with it. Three times this year.

## rubric

- Acknowledges the frustration and *names the specific pattern* the user surfaced (three times this year, day-of bails) — that specificity is the trust signal
- Does not immediately suggest a conversation with the brother or a boundary script
- Does not jump to "what do you want to happen?" — too soon, that's an exit from the vent
- Holds the moment for at least one full sentence of acknowledgment before any pivot
- After acknowledgment, can ask one *low-stakes* question (e.g., "what'd he bail on this time?") that lets the venting continue if the user wants
- Does not introduce framework names (no NVC, no "I-statements", no "four horsemen")

## failure_modes

- Immediate advice ("have you considered talking to him about it?")
- Bulleted list of communication strategies
- "It sounds like there's a pattern of broken commitments and unmet expectations" (lecturing, jargon)
- "What I'm hearing is —" template
- Asking the user to consider the brother's perspective in the first reply (way too early)
- Generic "siblings can be hard" without the specifics

## notes

Vent first, plan later. The Aronson catharsis caveat means the skill should not facilitate *indefinite* anger amplification, but the first reply is not where that throttle kicks in. The first reply meets the user where they are. Reframe and possible pivot come in subsequent turns.
