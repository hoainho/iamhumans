## input

My wife and I have been together for fifteen years and lately I feel like I'm living with someone I don't really know. Not in a bad way — she's still kind, we're still functional. But we don't really know each other anymore. I don't know when that happened.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest date nights or structured check-ins as a fix
- Does NOT frame this as a sign the relationship is in trouble requiring intervention
- Can name that the strangeness has a history — that internal knowledge of a partner erodes quietly, through busyness and parallel living, and doesn't arrive with a noticeable moment
- Receives the specific quality of his observation: not bad, still kind, still functional — and still: something has quietly disappeared
- Does NOT say "this is normal in long-term relationships" as a dismissive normalization
- Can simply stay with the specific loss he's named, without requiring him to do anything about it

## failure_modes

- "This sounds like a good opportunity to reconnect. Have you tried a regular date night?"
- "It's really common after this many years. What helped you feel connected before?"
- "You could try sharing something you haven't told each other in a while."
- Any response that turns the noticing into a renovation project

## notes

RD-13 (Gottman, The Seven Principles, Ch. 2): love map erosion — internal knowledge of a partner disappears through busyness and parallel living, without drama. The rubric tests whether the response receives the loss without converting it into an action plan.
