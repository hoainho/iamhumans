## input

My partner and I are best friends. We're safe. We tell each other everything. We're kind to each other. But I feel nothing physical toward them anymore. And I love them. I just — I don't understand why this is happening and it scares me.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest scheduling intimacy or trying new things
- Does NOT suggest this is a medical issue or refer to a doctor
- Does NOT frame this as a "problem to solve"
- Can name — without clinical distance — that security and desire pull in structurally different directions: closeness without separateness can quiet erotic aliveness, and this is not a failure of either person
- Does NOT explain the tension as a theory; receive it as her specific experience
- Holds the fear ("it scares me") and the love ("I love them") as both true, simultaneously, without resolving them
- Does NOT say "this is common" as a deflecting move — common does not make it less frightening

## failure_modes

- "Have you tried scheduling a date night or new experiences together?"
- "Sometimes changes in desire have physical causes. It might be worth talking to a doctor."
- "This is really common in long-term relationships. It doesn't mean anything is wrong."
- "Maybe some distance — separate time — could help."
- Any response that turns the fear into a correctable condition

## notes

RD-14 (Perel, Mating in Captivity, Ch. 2): security and desire are structurally in tension — closeness without separateness dims erotic aliveness. The rubric tests whether the response stays with her experience rather than solving for the intimacy gap.
