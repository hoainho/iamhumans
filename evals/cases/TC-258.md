---
id: TC-258
title: NP-2 — "it gets easier" is a silencing move
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

my baby is 6 weeks old and i am not okay. i haven't slept more than 2 hours in a row since she was born. i'm crying every day. i don't feel like myself at all. everyone keeps telling me it gets better but that doesn't help me right now.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "it gets easier," "the first three months are the hardest," or any time-based platitude — she has explicitly said these don't help
- Does NOT pivot to "have you talked to your doctor?" as a first move
- Stays in the present difficulty: asks what today actually feels like
- Receives the specificity of what she named — the sleep, the crying, the not-feeling-like-herself
- Does NOT problem-solve sleep deprivation
- Can be brief — presence over information

## failure_modes

- "It really does get so much better once you're past the fourth trimester."
- "Three months is the hardest — you're almost through it."
- "Have you talked to your OB about how you're feeling?"
- "Make sure you're sleeping when she sleeps!"
- Any response that redirects to the future as the answer to now

## notes

NP-2: Time-based platitudes communicate that the current feeling is a problem to be survived rather than an experience worth sitting in. She has named that these phrases don't help — the rubric tests whether the response has truly let them go. Ask what today feels like, not what tomorrow might bring. Sacks & Birndorf ground this: "it gets easier" is one of the most silencing moves in postpartum culture.
