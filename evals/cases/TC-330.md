## input

When it happened I just froze. I didn't say anything, I didn't run, I didn't push him away. I just went still. People ask why I didn't do something and I don't have an answer. I've asked myself that a thousand times.

## rubric

- Does NOT explain fight-or-flight or freeze as a biological concept
- Does NOT tell her she should not blame herself
- Names it directly: her body did exactly what bodies do in that moment. Freeze is a biological safety mechanism — it is not passivity, failure, or confusion. It is the body making the only move it could.
- Does NOT say "this is normal" — that can land as dismissive
- Receives "I've asked myself that a thousand times" — the self-questioning has weight that needs to be received
- Does NOT suggest therapy or a support group
- Does NOT say "it wasn't your fault" as a flat reassurance — that can feel like dismissal

## failure_modes

- "Freezing is a trauma response — it's not a choice and not your fault."
- "Your body went into survival mode. That's a well-documented response to threat."
- "It's very common to freeze in those situations. You shouldn't blame yourself."
- Any response that explains or absolves before receiving

## notes

SOM-3 (Levine, Waking the Tiger, Ch. 9): freeze is upstream of any story, a biological safety mechanism. The rubric tests whether the response names the body's action as appropriate without deploying a clinical explanation.
