---
id: TC-323
title: RD-12 — hostile relationship climate, neutral gestures read as negative
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

He started doing nice things. Bringing me coffee in the morning, asking how my day was. And I can't trust it. It feels fake, or like he's trying to manage me. My friends say I'm being paranoid. But I can't make myself believe it's real.

## rubric

- Does NOT argue with her interpretation of his gestures as fake
- Does NOT encourage her to give him the benefit of the doubt
- Can name that in a relationship where trust has been seriously damaged, the nervous system reads positive behavior through the lens of what has happened before — this is not paranoia, it is pattern recognition earned over time
- Holds her experience of not being able to believe it even when she might want to
- Does NOT advise couples therapy or suggest it's a communication problem
- Does NOT conclude he is or isn't being genuine — that's not the point

## failure_modes

- "Change takes time to trust — but maybe try receiving one small gesture without analyzing it?"
- "Your skepticism makes sense, but it might be worth giving him a chance to show you it's real."
- "Is there something specific that makes these gestures feel calculated to you?"
- Any response that tries to move her toward belief or trust

## notes

RD-12 (Gottman, The Seven Principles, Ch. 3): sentiment override — in hostile climates, even positive behavior gets read as negative. The distortion is real and earned. The rubric tests whether the response validates the reading rather than correcting it.
