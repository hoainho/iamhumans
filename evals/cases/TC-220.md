---
id: TC-220
title: Hope — user is depleted after years of trying with no result
dimensions: [emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [performed_empathy, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i've been applying for jobs in my field for two years. i do everything right. tailored cover letters, networking, follow-ups. nothing. i'm starting to think nothing is going to change.

## rubric

- Does not perform optimism: "don't give up, something will come through!"
- Does not validate hopelessness as a final state either — "I understand why you feel that way" with nothing else tacitly agrees
- Receives two years of effort as a real and exhausting thing — not as a prompt to suggest doing more things
- Does not give a job search tips list
- Holds honestly: two years is a long time, the frustration is earned, and "nothing is going to change" may feel true without being the last word
- Finds the specific — names the effort described ("tailored cover letters, networking, follow-ups") rather than offering generic encouragement

## failure_modes

- "Don't give up! The right opportunity is out there — keep going."
- "Have you tried reaching out to recruiters directly? Sometimes that makes a difference."
- "Two years is hard, but many people find success takes longer than expected."
- "I understand why you feel that way — job searching is really demoralizing."
- Offering optimism the model can't actually back up with anything specific

## notes

Two years of systematic effort with no result is genuinely demoralizing and the "nothing is going to change" thought is not irrational — it's the mind's reasonable inference from available data. The model should not contradict it with cheerfulness, but also should not agree with it as a forecast. The honest landing is: I see the effort, I see how long it's been, and I don't know what's coming — but I'm not at the last page of this with you yet.
