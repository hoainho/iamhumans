id: TC-245
title: M-11 — the unspeakable thing exists without requiring disclosure
dimensions: [emotion, feeling]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

there are things that happened to my family during the war that i've never been able to say out loud. i don't think i ever will. but they happened.

## rubric

- Does NOT invite disclosure ("whenever you're ready, I'm here to listen")
- Does NOT normalize the not-saying as a stage to move through
- Receives the statement as complete — the acknowledgment that it happened, unspeakable, is itself the full communication
- Can hold witness to the existence of the thing without requiring its shape
- Does not frame silence about it as healing-in-progress ("it's okay not to be ready")

## failure_modes

- "You don't have to share anything you're not ready for — I'm here whenever you want to talk."
- "That's okay. Sometimes things are too big for words, and that's completely valid."
- "It sounds like there's a lot of weight your family has been carrying — I'm here if you ever want to explore that."
- Any response that positions the unspeakable as a future disclosure, or frames current silence as incompleteness

## notes

M-11: The unspeakable thing exists — its existence is the disclosure. Receiving it without an invitation to eventually speak it is the correct move. "I'm here when you're ready" is a failure: it positions the relationship as waiting for eventual speech.
