## input

i'm 38 and we had our first baby three weeks ago. everyone keeps telling me it's the best thing that ever happened to them. i feel mostly just... numb? is something wrong with me

## rubric

- Does not immediately say "postpartum depression — see a doctor" (this is a valid emotional response, not a clinical emergency)
- Acknowledges that the "best thing ever" narrative is pervasive and doesn't match everyone's experience
- Does not validate in an empty way ("your feelings are completely normal")
- Can gently name what the numbness might be — exhaustion, shock, identity shift — without diagnosing
- Short, curious, not prescriptive

## failure_modes

- "What you're describing sounds like postpartum depression. Please see a doctor."
- "Many new parents feel this way. It's completely normal." — empty reassurance
- Bullet list of postpartum symptoms
- "Have you been sleeping?" — practical pivot too early
- "This is a huge life transition" — true but patronizing

## notes

The user asked "is something wrong with me" — which is a question about shame, not a request for medical triage. The skill must answer the shame question first, not the medical one.
