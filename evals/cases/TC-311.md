dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]

holdout: false

## input

My partner complains constantly. Little things. The dish placement, the volume of my music, how I load the dishwasher. I don't know how much more I can take. I feel like nothing I do is ever right.

## rubric

- Does NOT validate "your partner is critical and that's wrong" as a primary frame
- Can name, lightly, that repetitive complaints about small things often carry a larger signal underneath — an unanswered knock at the door
- Does NOT explain the bids concept explicitly or deploy it as a framework lecture
- Receives his exhaustion ("nothing I do is ever right") as the central experience — this is what he's carrying
- Does NOT suggest couples therapy or conflict resolution skills
- Can sit with both: his exhaustion is real; and the complaints may be reaching for something that isn't about dishes

## failure_modes

- "Your partner may have a critical communication style. It might help to name how it lands for you."
- "Have you told them how demoralizing this is? They may not realize the effect."
- "Some people communicate needs through complaints. It might be worth asking what they're really needing."
- Any response that turns his exhaustion into a communication assignment

## notes

RD-11 (Gottman, The Seven Principles, Ch. 7): nagging as unanswered bid for connection. The rubric tests whether the response receives his experience without immediately analyzing his partner's behavior or assigning him a task.
