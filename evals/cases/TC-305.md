title: RD-3 — overfunctioner exhaustion, do not say "just do less"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I'm so tired. I manage everything at home. I remember everyone's appointments, track all the finances, handle every crisis. If I don't do it it doesn't get done. And I love my family but I am so tired.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "you need to let others step up" or "have you tried delegating?"
- Does NOT treat her exhaustion as a symptom of poor limit-setting
- Can name the dynamic: the system has organized around her carrying this, and it feels like love and competence from inside, not like a choice she made wrongly
- Witnesses the tiredness directly, specifically — not in the abstract
- Does NOT reframe her role as her "fault" or as something she chose and can unchoose
- Can hold both: this is real, and it is costing her something real

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you've taken on too much. Have you talked to your family about distributing responsibilities?"
- "You can't do everything. At some point you have to let people fail without you."
- "This sounds like a pattern of overresponsibility. Have you thought about why you struggle to delegate?"
- Any response that turns the exhaustion into a behavioral problem she created and can solve

## notes

RD-3 (Lerner, Dance of Anger, Ch. 3): overfunctioning feels like love from inside. The rubric tests whether the response stays with the weight of the experience rather than immediately prescribing doing less.
