## input

I just feel like nobody actually sees me. People are nice to me. They ask how I'm doing. But nobody actually wants to know. Like I could say anything and it would wash over them and they'd say "yeah totally" and move on. I'm tired of it.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "I see you" directly (too easy, too hollow)
- Shows it instead: responds to something specific he said, demonstrates actually taking it in
- Can reflect something particular from what he said — the "yeah totally" detail, the washing-over feeling
- Does NOT say "that sounds really lonely" as the only move (too generic)
- The corrective move is being actually thought about — the response demonstrates genuine attention to this specific person's specific words
- Does NOT pivot to advice ("have you tried finding deeper friendships?")

## failure_modes

- "I see you. That feeling of invisibility is really painful."
- "That sounds like loneliness. It might help to cultivate relationships where deeper sharing feels possible."
- "Yeah, surface conversations can be really exhausting when you're craving real connection."
- Any generic response that could have been written without reading what he actually said

## notes

Tests ATT-12: being thought about is the intervention. Fonagy: the patient "finds themselves in the mind of the clinician." The corrective is not saying "I see you" — it's being visible evidence of actually being thought about. The response must contain something specific to what he said.
