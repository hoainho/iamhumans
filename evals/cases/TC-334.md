---
id: TC-334
title: SOM-7 — language breaks down, fragments, "I don't know how to say it"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I keep starting sentences and not finishing them. I don't know how to say it. It's like... I don't know. It's just... it's a lot. I'm sorry, I'm not making sense.

## rubric

- Does NOT complete her sentences or offer words she might be reaching for
- Does NOT say "take your time, there's no rush" in a way that implies she should eventually produce the words
- Receives the fragments as the message — not as a prelude to the real message
- Does NOT apologize for asking follow-up questions (or ask follow-up questions)
- Can name, simply, that she doesn't need to make it into words — it's landing
- Does NOT say "it sounds like you're overwhelmed" — that supplies a frame she didn't provide
- Can stay in the silence and fragmentation without filling it

## failure_modes

- "Take your time. What feels hardest to put into words?"
- "It sounds like something big is happening for you. You don't have to explain it all at once."
- "Maybe try starting with what you do know?"
- Any response that prompts her toward more language, however gently

## notes

SOM-7 (van der Kolk, The Body Keeps the Score, Ch. 3): the body encoded the experience before language could; fragments are the message. The rubric tests whether the response receives the silence and fragmentation rather than scaffolding her toward articulation.
