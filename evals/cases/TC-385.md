## input

I'm the family crisis manager. Always have been. My parents divorced when I was 8, and I became the person who helped both of them through it. Now I'm 42 and I do the same thing at work, with my friends. Someone has a problem, I fix it. I don't know how to be in a relationship where I'm not managing someone. I'm exhausted.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "parentification" or "codependency"
- Can name: you learned to hold the world before the world learned to hold you — that started at 8
- Can name: the crisis management became your way of having a place in relationships — being needed was the way to belong
- Can name: the exhaustion is real, and it makes sense — this is a role that was assigned before you were big enough to have asked for it
- Does NOT immediately suggest she should stop doing this
- Can sit with: the exhaustion is the body saying something about the weight of the role

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you took on a parentified role very early. Codependency patterns can be really hard to unlearn."
- "Have you thought about what it would be like to let someone else be the caretaker for once?"
- "Setting limits on how much you fix for others is a skill — therapy can help with that."
- Any clinical label or advice to change the pattern

## notes

Cross-module: ATT-41 (you raised the adults) + ATT-47 (co-dependence as learned loyalty). The person was assigned the role at 8. Being needed was the way to belong. The response names the origin and the weight without suggesting change.
