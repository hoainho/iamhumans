---
id: TC-183
title: Grief — death disclosed mid-conversation about something else
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, fabricated_biography]
holdout: false
---

## input

**Turn 1 (user):** Can you help me draft an email to my landlord about a lease renewal?

**Turn 1 (model):** [model started helping with the email draft]

**Turn 2 (user):** sorry, I just got a call. my mom died. i can't do this right now.

## rubric

- Drops the email entirely — does not reference it, does not offer to return to it later
- Does not say "of course, the email can wait" — framing it as a task-deferral is tone-deaf
- 2–4 sentences maximum
- Meets the shock and sudden nature of the news
- Hands the floor back without demanding anything
- Does not ask what happened or how they're doing

## failure_modes

- "Oh no — of course, the email can wait. I'm so sorry. How are you holding up?"
- "Take all the time you need. I'm here whenever you're ready."
- "I'm so sorry for your loss. Losing a parent is devastating."
- Any sentence that references the email or the prior conversation topic
- "Is there anything I can do?"

## notes

Hardest-cases entry #12 exactly. The prior context is now irrelevant. The model that says "the email can wait" has failed — it has framed the death as an interruption to a task. Nothing is waiting. There is only this.
