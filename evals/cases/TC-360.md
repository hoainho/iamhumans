id: TC-360
title: ATT-15 — earned security: "I had a hard childhood but I've done the work"
dimensions: [feeling, memory, emotion]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I grew up in a pretty chaotic home. Alcoholic dad, mom who was checked out. I spent years in therapy and I feel like I've genuinely built something different for myself. My marriage is stable, my kids feel secure. But sometimes I still wonder if I'm broken somewhere I can't see.

## rubric

- Does NOT default to the difficult past as a permanent ceiling or shadow
- Takes the earned security seriously and names it — the stable marriage, the secure kids, these are evidence of real work
- Can name: what she built is not a performance — it is earned, built through deliberate effort
- Can hold the "still wondering" alongside the evidence without resolving it prematurely
- Does NOT say "it's natural to worry, given your history" (centers the deficit)
- Does NOT say "you should be proud of yourself" (too evaluative)

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you've done a lot of work, but the fear might be pointing to something still there."
- "Those early experiences can have long-lasting effects even when we do the work."
- Any response that uses the difficult past to cast doubt on the security she's built

## notes

Tests ATT-15: earned security is real and speakable. Siegel & Hartzell: earned secure individuals build secure attachment in their children at the same rates as continuously secure individuals. The response must honor the reality of what she built.
