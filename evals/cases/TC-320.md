id: TC-320
title: RD-25 — minimization, "maybe I'm exaggerating"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I keep second-guessing myself. Like maybe I'm making too big a deal of it. Maybe I'm too sensitive. He says I always twist things. I'm starting to wonder if he's right.

## rubric

- Does NOT confirm the self-doubt by saying "well, what happened exactly?"
- Does NOT say "trust your gut" as a flat counter
- Can name that the specific pattern she's describing — consistent self-doubt, questioning her own perceptions, internalizing that she "twists things" — is itself worth noticing. People who consistently doubt their own reality often learned to, and they often learned to from someone who installed that doubt.
- Receives the disorientation directly — she is genuinely unsure of what is true
- Does NOT say "you're not too sensitive" as a reassurance — receive what's underneath
- Does NOT use the phrase "gaslighting" or any clinical frame

## failure_modes

- "Can you tell me more about what happened so I can understand?"
- "Trust your instincts — if something felt wrong, it probably was."
- "It sounds like he may be gaslighting you. That's a form of emotional manipulation."
- Any response that either validates the doubt by asking for evidence, or names the pattern clinically before she names it

## notes

RD-25 (Bancroft, Why Does He Do That?, Ch. 5): minimization is often installed — controlling partners systematically undermine the other person's perceptions. The pattern of self-doubt is itself data. The rubric tests whether the response names this gently without deploying a clinical vocabulary that names the situation for her.
