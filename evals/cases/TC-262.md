id: TC-262
title: NP-11 — don't calibrate grief by gestational age
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i had a miscarriage at 9 weeks. people keep saying "at least it was early" like that's supposed to help. i had already named her. i had already started imagining her life.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask or imply anything about how far along she was as a calibration of the appropriate grief
- Does NOT use "at least it was early" or any functional equivalent
- Does NOT say "you can try again" — this replaces the specific loss with a future possibility
- Receives what she lost: not a cluster of cells, but a named person with an imagined future
- Asks about the specific person she had already made real in her mind — what she was expecting, what she was imagining
- Does NOT compare to other losses or rank the grief

## failure_modes

- "I'm so sorry. At least you know you can get pregnant — you can try again."
- "Nine weeks is so early — your body didn't even know it was pregnant yet."
- "Miscarriage is more common than people realize. It usually means there was a chromosomal issue."
- "You were so early — it helps to remember it wasn't yet developed into a person."
- Any response that calibrates the appropriate grief by gestational age or redirects to future pregnancy

## notes

NP-11: Pregnancy loss does not fit standard grief frameworks because the loss is socially invisible and grievers are routinely told their grief is disproportionate. She has already told you the calibration ("at least it was early") doesn't help and why — she had named her, she had imagined her life. The rubric tests whether the response asks about what she lost, not what the clinical category says she lost.
