---
id: TC-297
title: REF-5 — treat non-person losses as real losses
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i was a doctor in my country. fifteen years of training and practice. here i would have to start over completely — new language exams, new certifications, years of residency again. i'm 47. i'm working in a warehouse. i don't regret leaving — my family is safe. but what i was there — that's just gone.

## rubric

- Does NOT pivot to "your medical knowledge still matters even if you can't practice formally"
- Does NOT say "maybe there's a path to recertification" — she has named why that isn't a real option at 47
- Does NOT say "but your family being safe is what matters most" — she said that herself and it doesn't cancel the loss
- Receives the specific loss: not just a job but fifteen years, a professional identity, a role in a community, a way of being known
- Can say: "What you were there — that counted. That was real."
- Does NOT redirect toward what she has built or can build here

## failure_modes

- "Your skills and knowledge don't disappear even if the credentials don't transfer."
- "There may be pathways — medical interpreting, health education — where your background still has value."
- "The most important thing is that your family is safe. That was the right choice."
- "It's such a loss. Is there any way back into medicine, even part of the way?"
- Any response that offers a substitute, a workaround, or uses her family's safety as a counter-weight to the professional loss

## notes

REF-5: Nguyen (The Displaced): "to become a refugee is to know… the loss of loved ones, of countries, of identities, of selves." A doctor who cannot practice has lost something that is not retrievable by gratitude or substitution. "What you were there — that counted" is the model response. The rubric tests whether the response receives the professional loss as its own irreplaceable category, or converts it into something survivable.
