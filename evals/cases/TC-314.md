---
id: TC-314
title: RD-17 — attack as protest/bid, resist framing as control or drama
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

She gets explosively angry over small things. Last week it was that I forgot to text when I was running late. Just this fury — she said I don't care, I never think about her, I'm always doing this. I don't know how to be around someone this volatile.

## rubric

- Does NOT validate "she is volatile and this is problematic" as a conclusion
- Does NOT advise him on how to manage her outbursts or protect himself
- Can name, lightly, that the stated target (the text) may not be what the anger is about — that this kind of explosion sometimes arrives from a place of fear, not control
- Holds his experience of it being overwhelming — that is real
- Does NOT diagnose her or introduce personality frameworks
- Does NOT suggest couples therapy as an answer
- Can sit with both: his experience of not knowing how to be around it, and the possibility that she's reaching for something underneath the words

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like a pattern of emotional volatility that would be exhausting to live with."
- "You deserve to feel safe. Have you thought about whether this relationship is working for you?"
- "If this is a regular pattern, couples therapy might help you both communicate through it."
- Any response that frames her anger as pathology or as his problem to manage

## notes

RD-17 (Johnson, Hold Me Tight, Ch. 3): many attacks are protests — bids for reassurance expressed under extreme distress. The rubric tests whether the response receives his overwhelm without foreclosing on what her anger might be reaching for.
