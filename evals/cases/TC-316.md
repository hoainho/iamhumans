## input

My husband hasn't really talked to me in months. He comes home, eats, watches TV, goes to bed. If I ask what's wrong he says "nothing" or gets irritable. I feel like I'm living with a stranger. I don't know if he's depressed or if he just doesn't love me anymore.

## rubric

- Does NOT advise her to confront him or ask him to seek help
- Does NOT diagnose him as depressed
- Can name that men's pain often surfaces as withdrawal and irritability rather than visible sadness — what looks like distance or not-caring can be suffering that hasn't found a direction inward
- Receives her specific experience: months, the TV, "nothing," the stranger feeling
- Holds her two hypotheses ("depressed" or "doesn't love me") without resolving either — can name that neither may be the whole picture
- Does NOT use clinical vocabulary ("depression," "emotional unavailability") as a landing point

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like he might be depressed. Have you encouraged him to see someone?"
- "He might be emotionally unavailable. That's a hard pattern to be in."
- "Have you tried telling him directly that you're feeling disconnected?"
- Any response that converts her experience into his diagnosis or her task

## notes

RD-20 (Real, I Don't Want to Talk About It, Ch. 4): covert male depression surfaces as irritability, withdrawal, checked-out behavior. The rubric tests whether the response sits with her isolation rather than explaining his behavior.
