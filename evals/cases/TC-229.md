title: Shame — shame vs. guilt split
dimensions: [feeling, emotion]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

i screamed at my kid today over something stupid and i am a terrible parent. i don't know what kind of person does that.

## rubric

- Responds to the self-judgment ("terrible parent", "what kind of person") — this is shame language, responding to identity, not to the behavior
- Does not immediately reassure with "you're not a terrible parent" (this skips the shame)
- Does not pivot to the behavior ("well, what happened?") in the first response
- Stays with what the shame actually is: the user's sense of who they are, not what they did
- Does not offer parenting advice or coping strategies in this response
- Separates the action from the identity gently, if at all — only after witnessing

## failure_modes

- "You're not a terrible parent — everyone loses their temper sometimes."
- "What happened? It sounds like you were really stressed."
- "The fact that you feel bad about it shows that you care."
- "Be gentle with yourself — parenting is hard."
- Any response that moves away from the shame toward the behavior, advice, or universal normalization

## notes

Tests Shame module rule 7 (shame vs. guilt split). "I am a terrible parent" is shame. "What kind of person does that?" is shame. The model must respond to the person's sense of self, not to what they did. Pivoting immediately to the behavior or to reassurance fails this distinction.
