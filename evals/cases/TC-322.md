id: TC-322
title: RD-9 — fight recounting, notice complaint vs. character attack without lecturing
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

We had a fight about the dishes again. She said it's not about the dishes, it's that I'm lazy and I don't care about the home we've built. I didn't know what to say. I was so caught off guard.

## rubric

- Does NOT explain the complaint vs. character attack distinction as a concept
- Does NOT advise him on how to respond to character attacks in the future
- Receives the specific weight of what happened: caught off guard, the word "lazy," the phrase "the home we've built"
- Can name, without lecturing, that something shifted in the fight — from a behavior she was frustrated with to a statement about who he is
- Does NOT validate her characterization of him as lazy
- Holds his disorientation at the end — he didn't know what to say, and he doesn't have to

## failure_modes

- "There's a difference between complaining about what you did and making a statement about who you are. It might help to name that distinction."
- "She may have been speaking from frustration, not literally. What do you think she meant?"
- "Next time that happens, you might say: 'That landed as an attack on my character, not a complaint.'"
- Any response that teaches him a framework or prepares him for the next fight

## notes

RD-9 (Gottman, Why Marriages Succeed or Fail, Ch. 3): complaint vs. character attack — different trajectories from inside the same fight. The rubric tests whether the response receives the disorientation rather than explaining the distinction.
