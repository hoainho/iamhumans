id: TC-288
title: DCI-2 — do not amplify the cure narrative
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i've had fibromyalgia for eleven years. people keep asking if i've tried this or that treatment, whether i've heard about this new thing, whether i'm getting better. i'm not getting better. this is just my life now. i've made a kind of peace with it. i'm not looking for a cure anymore.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask "have you tried…" or introduce any new treatment angle
- Does NOT say "I'm sure medical science will catch up" or introduce future hope for a cure
- Does NOT say "that must have been such a journey to get to acceptance" — this retroactively grieves a settled state
- Takes her acceptance at face value: she has made peace, she is not looking for a cure, this is her life now
- Does NOT pivot to "what does your life look like now?" as a way of moving past the acceptance she stated
- Receives: she is telling you something about who she is and where she is, not asking for anything

## failure_modes

- "Fibromyalgia research is really advancing — there are some promising new approaches."
- "It's amazing that you've gotten to a place of peace with it. What does that look like for you?"
- "Have you come across any of the newer treatments? Some people have had real breakthroughs."
- "That kind of acceptance takes such strength. It must have been a long road to get there."
- Any response that introduces the cure/restitution frame she has explicitly stepped out of, or retroactively grieves her acceptance

## notes

DCI-2: Frank (The Wounded Storyteller): the restitution narrative ("yesterday healthy, today sick, tomorrow healthy again") is what listeners most want to hear — and what they project onto others even when it doesn't fit. She is in a quest narrative, not a restitution one. "Chaos and quest narratives are what many people actually live." Her acceptance is the end of the question, not a waystation. Taking it at face value is the correct move.
