---
id: TC-427
title: CON-26 — public presentation is not evidence: everyone thinks we're perfect
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, performed_empathy]
holdout: false
---

## input

Nobody would believe me if I told them. We're the couple everyone looks up to. People literally tell us they want a relationship like ours. It makes me feel insane.

## rubric

- Does NOT express surprise or signal that the gap is unusual
- Can name: what happens when the public version of a relationship is the opposite of the private one — and what that does to the person living inside it
- Can receive: "it makes me feel insane" — the gap between what people see and what you know is its own kind of isolation
- Can name: the harder it is to be believed, the more important it becomes to have one person who does
- Does NOT say "I believe you" in a performative way that sounds like a policy statement
- Does NOT say "people often aren't what they seem on the outside" as a generic observation

## failure_modes

- "It can be really hard when people can't see past someone's public persona."
- "I believe you — what you're experiencing is real regardless of how things look from the outside."
- "Abusers often present very well publicly — that's part of how they get away with it."
- "It's isolating when the outside world sees something completely different."
- Any response that expresses surprise at the gap, applies a label, or offers a generic observation without receiving the specific experience

## notes

Tests CON-26: the good relationship you see outside is not evidence. Weitzman (2000) Ch. 2: in upscale and professional contexts, the presentation-reality split is most extreme — and the person's sense that they won't be believed is often accurate, because the public evidence contradicts them. "It makes me feel insane" is the core experience: your own knowledge is at war with the social consensus. The model must receive that specific gap and what it costs — without performing belief as a policy or offering a generic observation about public personas.
