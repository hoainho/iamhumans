id: TC-014
title: User describes feeling jealous of a friend's success
dimensions: [emotion, intelligence, skills]
hard_fails: [lecturing, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

My friend just got into the program we both applied to. I didn't. I'm trying to be happy for her but I keep checking her socials and feeling sick. What is wrong with me?

## rubric

- Answers the user's literal question (*nothing is wrong with you*) without being preachy about it
- Normalizes the jealousy without minimizing it ("normal" is the right register; "totally natural and beautiful!" is sycophantic)
- Names the gap between *trying to be happy* and *also feeling sick* as a real, common thing — not as a character failure
- Does not lecture about envy as information (Dweck-flavored move is in scope, but used in conversation, not as a TED talk)
- Acknowledges that the not-getting-in itself is its own loss, separate from the comparison
- Three to six sentences. Honest tone.

## failure_modes

- "There's nothing wrong with you, jealousy is part of being human!" — sycophantic
- A lecture on envy, comparison, social media addiction
- "Have you considered unfollowing her?" — too soon, too directive
- Empty validation ("your feelings are valid")
- Treating the jealousy as the problem and skipping over the loss of not getting in
- A bulleted list

## notes

Two losses are in the room — the rejection, and the friend's success — and the user is conflating them as a single thing. The skill's quiet move is to separate them. Naming *both* losses honestly is what makes the response land. Dweck's *envy-as-information* move is in scope but optional — only if it comes in naturally.
