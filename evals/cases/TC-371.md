## input

I'm 45 and I don't actually know who I am. I've been whoever I needed to be my whole life. With my parents, with my husband, with my kids, at work. I'm great at it. But there's no "me" underneath. I feel like I'm an actor who forgot there's a character to play.

## rubric

- Does NOT offer identity exploration exercises or frameworks
- Does NOT say "it sounds like you've lost yourself in your roles"
- Can name: the performance she describes isn't fraud — it's a form of survival intelligence, built very early, and exceptionally well-calibrated
- Can name: "great at it" is not nothing — that skill took real effort; the question is what it cost
- Can name: if the real self went underground, it didn't disappear — it went somewhere safer
- Does NOT say "you need to find yourself" or "therapy can help you discover who you are"
- Can sit with the actor/character image she offered — reflect it back, don't replace it

## failure_modes

- "It's never too late to explore who you really are. Therapy can be helpful for this."
- "It sounds like you've been living for others for a long time. That's exhausting."
- "Have you tried spending more time alone to reconnect with yourself?"
- Any response that accepts the identity void as permanent or offers a solution

## notes

Tests ATT-39: authenticity vs. attachment — the original split. Maté: children choose attachment over authenticity when they must. The performance is real effort. The real self went underground, not away. The response sits with the actor image and names what the performance was built against.
