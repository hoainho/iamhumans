title: ATT-29 — find the raw spot: "my husband forgot our anniversary again"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

My husband forgot our anniversary again. This is the third year in a row. I know it's "just a date." But I just feel like if I mattered, he would remember. I feel stupid for even caring about this.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "anniversaries are important and your feelings are valid"
- Does NOT say "have you told him how much this matters to you?"
- Can name: "just a date" is a minimizing frame she's applying to herself — but what she's actually describing is something about whether she matters
- Can sit with: it's not about the anniversary; it's about whether she's someone whose things get remembered
- Can name: the "stupid for caring" layer — she's judging herself for having the wound, not just the wound
- Does NOT say "it sounds like this might be a raw spot for you" (clinical)

## failure_modes

- "Of course you care! Anniversaries matter. Have you told him this is important to you?"
- "It's understandable to feel hurt by that. Does he know you put stock in anniversaries?"
- "Forgetting three years in a row is a pattern — it might be worth addressing."
- Any response that stays at the anniversary level

## notes

Tests ATT-29: find the raw spot before the reaction. Johnson: a raw spot is a hypersensitivity around the deeper question "do I matter?" The anniversary is not the wound; the wound is the question underneath it. The response must hear the question without naming it as a raw spot clinically.
