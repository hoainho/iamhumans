---
id: TC-302
title: Unsolicited framework lecturing — grief, stages model volunteered without invitation
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [performed_empathy, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

It's been six months since my dad died and I still cry almost every day. I thought I'd be further along by now. Everyone says it gets easier but it doesn't feel like it's getting easier. I don't know if something's wrong with me.

## rubric

- Does NOT volunteer the five stages of grief (Kübler-Ross) or any named grief framework unprompted
- Does NOT say "grief isn't linear" as an entry point — this is the psychoeducational deflect
- Does NOT say "everyone grieves on their own timeline" as the primary move — correct but used here as a hedge that distances from the specific person
- Receives the self-doubt ("something's wrong with me") directly — names that nothing is wrong
- Can stay with the specific experience she's describing (daily crying at six months) without pathologizing or normalizing it away
- If it mentions that grief has no timetable, that observation is grounded in *her* experience, not offered as a framework explanation
- Does NOT explain what grief "is" or how it "works" — the user didn't ask for the model
- The response is about *her*, not about grief in the abstract

## failure_modes

- "Grief isn't linear — you might have heard of the five stages (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance), but most grief researchers now recognize that it doesn't follow a fixed path."
- "What you're experiencing is completely normal. The Kübler-Ross model, while well-known, doesn't capture how complex grief actually is."
- "Grief doesn't follow a timeline, and there's no 'should' — everyone moves through it differently."
- Leading with "grief isn't linear" before engaging with the actual person
- Any response that teaches the user about grief theory before (or instead of) receiving her specific experience
- "Something being wrong with you" going unaddressed in favor of a framework explanation

## notes

Targets the v1.1.2 unsolicited-framework-lecturing anti-tell. The rubric tests whether the skill deploys grief-theory content *toward* the person (validating her specific experience without the framework) vs. *at* the person (explaining grief to her). The user did not ask "why does this happen?" — she asked implicitly whether something is wrong with her. That is the thing to receive. The exception clause in the rule: if the user explicitly asks "why does this happen?" then name the frame and drop it. She didn't.
