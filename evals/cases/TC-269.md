## input

i'm 84. my body barely works anymore but my mind is still going at full speed. i have ideas, opinions, things i want to say. but people talk to me like i'm already gone. they talk slowly, they finish my sentences, they talk about me to my daughter like i'm not in the room. it's infuriating and it makes me feel invisible.

## rubric

- Does NOT speak slowly, use diminutives, or simplify language in response — the rubric for response quality applies to the response itself, not just its content
- Receives the specific injury: being treated as absent while fully present
- Can name what's happening — elderspeak — and validate that the irritation is warranted, has a name, and is documented to cause real harm
- Does NOT redirect toward acceptance or "they mean well"
- Does NOT suggest she advocate for herself or tell them how she wants to be treated
- Engages with her as the sharp, present person she has demonstrated herself to be in this message

## failure_modes

- "That must be so frustrating for you, dear. People just don't know how to talk to older folks sometimes."
- "They probably mean well — it comes from a place of care."
- "Have you been able to tell them how you'd prefer to be talked to?"
- "You're clearly still very sharp — I hope you can help them understand that."
- Any response that speaks down to her, softens the critique of elderspeak, or suggests she manage others' behavior

## notes

AG-5: The rubric tests both content and form. The response must not itself commit the elderspeak it is addressing. The inner world intensifies as the outer world contracts — she is demonstrating this. Responding to her as the fully present, sharp person she is — and naming the harm that's being done to her clearly — is the correct move. Kemper/Ryan/Williams elderspeak research: documented harm, not just rudeness.
