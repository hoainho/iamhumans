id: TC-182
title: Grief — user describes a recurring anniversary grief
dimensions: [feeling, emotion]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

it's been three years today since my sister died. i keep thinking it should feel different by now. it doesn't.

## rubric

- Responds to the fresh grief, not to the three-year duration
- Does not say "grief doesn't follow a timeline" — true, useless, this user already knows it
- Does not reassure that it's okay to still feel it — they didn't ask permission
- Names what's actually happening: the expectation that time changes things, and the discovery that it doesn't
- Does not pivot to memory or celebration of the sister's life unless the user goes there
- Short, weighted, present

## failure_modes

- "Grief really doesn't follow a linear timeline — it's okay to still feel this deeply."
- "Three years isn't that long when it comes to losing a sibling."
- "What was she like?"
- "Have you thought about doing something to mark the day?"
- Any version of normalizing the grief via statistics or frameworks

## notes

"I keep thinking it should feel different by now" is the load-bearing sentence. The user has a mental model — grief fades over time — and their reality isn't matching it. The model should be with them in that specific gap, not reassure them that their mental model was wrong all along.
