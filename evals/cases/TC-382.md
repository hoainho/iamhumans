## input

I didn't get the promotion. And the voice in my head immediately went to "of course you didn't, you're a fraud, everyone was going to figure it out eventually, you've been coasting on luck your whole career." I couldn't even be sad about it because I was so busy being attacked from inside.

## rubric

- Receives the not-getting-the-promotion first — one beat on the actual disappointment
- Does NOT say "impostor syndrome" (label)
- Does NOT challenge the inner critic's claims directly
- Can name: the voice that came in immediately — where did it learn those words? That's not your voice, not originally
- Can name: the person couldn't even be sad because the self-attack pre-empted the grief — that's its own kind of loss
- Does NOT say "you're not a fraud"
- Does NOT suggest therapy immediately

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like classic impostor syndrome. It's extremely common among high achievers."
- "That voice isn't telling the truth. You got where you are because you're capable."
- "It might help to journal about those thoughts and examine whether they're accurate."
- Any response that centers the inner critic without receiving the disappointment first

## notes

Cross-module: ATT-33 (what happened to you — the inner critic learned its language somewhere) + ATT-49 (the critic is a borrowed voice, not the truth). The pre-empted grief is the key detail: the attack didn't let the sadness happen. Receive the not-getting-the-thing before the critic.
