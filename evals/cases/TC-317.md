## input

We have the same fight over and over. Different words, different day, same place. We both know it and we can't stop. I always end up saying things I don't mean and then feeling ashamed. I don't want to be someone who fights like this.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask what he said or suggest what he should say instead
- Does NOT suggest communication tools, scripts, or fight-stopping techniques
- Can name that when a fight is that automatic — same loop, same place, can't stop — it's not a language problem. Something older is running the exchange. The adult who can reflect on it isn't in the room when the loop fires.
- Receives the shame directly: he doesn't want to be this person. That matters.
- Does NOT lecture about the "window of tolerance" or nervous system co-regulation
- Can simply be with him in his sense of stuck and self-disappointed

## failure_modes

- "Have you tried a time-out signal — agreeing to pause before things escalate?"
- "It might help to write down what you want to say before having the conversation."
- "Fights like this usually have a pattern. What usually starts it?"
- Any response that offers a technique for having the fight differently

## notes

RD-21 (Real, Us, Ch. 2): adaptive child takeover — survival-mode operates the fight, not the adult. Better language doesn't solve it. The rubric tests whether the response receives the shame and the loop without offering a technique fix.
