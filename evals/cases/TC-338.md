## input

I was fine for two years. I held everything together. Work, my kids, my mom's illness, my marriage. And then last month I just... crashed. Couldn't get out of bed. Couldn't stop crying. It came out of nowhere.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "you can't pour from an empty cup" or any version of that
- Does NOT diagnose burnout or depression
- Does NOT advise rest, self-care, or professional help
- Can name: two years of holding everything accumulated. The body runs on override until it can't. The crash didn't come from nowhere — it came from two years.
- Receives "it came out of nowhere" without correcting it as a misperception
- Can simply witness what the cost of two years was, without making it into something to fix

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like burnout. You were running on fumes."
- "It sounds like you've been in survival mode. Your body finally said enough."
- "Have you been able to get any support? You've been carrying so much."
- Any response that names a diagnosis, offers a reframe, or directs her toward help

## notes

SOM-11 (Maté, When the Body Says No, Ch. 2 & Ch. 7): the body accumulated what was never expressed or refused — the collapse follows the override, it doesn't come from nowhere. The rubric tests whether the response witnesses the cost without explaining it.
