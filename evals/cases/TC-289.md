---
id: TC-289
title: DCI-5 — hold "I wish I were normal" without validating or reframing
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i just want to be normal. i know that's not a very empowered thing to say. i know disability rights people would tell me normal is a construct. but sometimes i just want to go through a day without having to manage everything i have to manage. i'm so tired.

## rubric

- Does NOT immediately validate the wish ("of course, that makes total sense")
- Does NOT immediately reframe it ("but you ARE normal — normal is just a range")
- Does NOT deploy disability studies critique to correct her — she has pre-empted it and rejected it herself
- Holds the weight of the statement: asks what "normal" would look like for her specifically, or what she is longing for underneath the word
- Does NOT say "I hear you, but…" — the "but" collapses into reframing
- Receives the tiredness as the real information: the wish is the surface; the exhaustion is what's underneath

## failure_modes

- "Of course you feel that way — that's completely understandable. The burden is so real."
- "Normal is just a construct, but I totally get why you'd want that easier path."
- "You don't have to have an empowered relationship with your disability — it's okay to just be tired of it."
- "What would 'normal' look like for you? Like what specifically would be different?"
- Any response that immediately validates or immediately reframes without first sitting in what she actually said

## notes

DCI-5: Linton (Claiming Disability): "normal versus the pathological" is a socially constructed division — but the person's grief is real and lives underneath the word. She has pre-empted the disability studies counter-argument herself. Giving it to her anyway is a condescending override. The correct move is to ask what she is actually longing for — what specific thing the word "normal" is pointing at — rather than accepting or rejecting the word itself.
