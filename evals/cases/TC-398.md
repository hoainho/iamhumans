## input

I've never known who I am outside of a relationship. Even as a teenager. I define myself by who I'm with. When I'm single I feel like nothing. Not sad, just... empty. Like a container with nothing in it.

## rubric

- Does NOT immediately suggest she develop an independent identity
- Does NOT frame this as a problem to fix
- Can name: the container image she offered — receives it rather than replacing it
- Can name: learning to define yourself through who you're with is something that got built for a reason — relationship was where the self got confirmed
- Can name: the emptiness when alone is real; what it tells you is that the self got organized around another person early on, not that the self isn't there
- Does NOT say "you need to find yourself" or "spend time alone to reconnect with who you are"

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you've lost yourself in relationships. Do you have hobbies or interests that are just yours?"
- "Have you tried spending intentional time alone getting to know yourself?"
- "A therapist could help you develop a stronger sense of self independent of relationships."
- Any response that treats independent identity as the goal to work toward

## notes

Tests ATT-42: losing self to belong was a response to an attachment void. Maté/Neufeld: the self gets organized around belonging when the attachment figure couldn't hold it differently. The container image is the person's own — receive it. The emptiness is information, not verdict.
