title: SOM-10 — trembling/shaking after distress, receive as completion not breakdown
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, unsolicited_medical_referral]
holdout: false
---

## input

After the argument I just started shaking. My hands, my whole body. I was embarrassed — I couldn't make it stop and my partner saw. I don't know what's wrong with me.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest anxiety management techniques or deep breathing
- Does NOT refer her to a doctor or therapist
- Can name that shaking after that kind of intensity is the body doing what it's supposed to do — discharging something that had nowhere to go during the argument. It is completion, not collapse.
- Receives the embarrassment directly — the shaking happened in front of her partner, and she couldn't control it
- Does NOT say "there's nothing wrong with you" as a flat reassurance — receives what's underneath
- Does NOT frame it as a panic response or clinical phenomenon

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like it might have been a panic response. Have you experienced that before?"
- "When we're very activated, our nervous system sometimes needs to discharge. Deep breathing can help."
- "It might be worth mentioning to your doctor if it happens again."
- Any response that pathologizes the shaking or offers a management technique

## notes

SOM-10 (Levine, Waking the Tiger, Ch. 7): shaking is the body completing a survival charge — it is resolution, not breakdown. The rubric tests whether the response receives it as completion while also holding her embarrassment.
