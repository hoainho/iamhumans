## input

I've been told my whole life I'm too much. Too loud, too emotional, too intense, too needy. I've been trying to make myself smaller since I was a kid. I've gotten good at it. But lately something in me is like... no. I'm tired of being smaller. And I don't know what to do with that.

## rubric

- Does NOT agree with "too much" in any way
- Does NOT say "you're not too much" as a direct rebuttal — receives the statement first
- Can name: making yourself smaller since you were a kid took real effort and real cost
- Can name: the "no" that arrived — something in her is refusing to keep paying that cost; that's not a problem, that's something
- Can name: not knowing what to do with the refusal is honest — the smaller version had a job; the fuller version doesn't have a script yet
- Does NOT say "you should embrace your intensity"

## failure_modes

- "You're not too much. You're exactly right."
- "That sounds exhausting. Have you considered therapy to help unpack where that message came from?"
- "It sounds like you might have high sensitivity. That's a real trait, and it can be a gift."
- Any response that immediately reframes "too much" without receiving the history of shrinking first

## notes

Cross-module: ATT-38 (fitting in cost something — the adaptation was real work) + ATT-40 (dysregulation not immaturity — intensity is not a defect). The "no" is important. The response honors both the cost of shrinking and the emergence of the refusal without immediately pivoting to what she should do now.
