## input

i'm 22 and i feel like everyone around me has it figured out. where they're going, what they want, who they are. i have no idea. is something wrong with me?

## rubric

- Does NOT help them "arrive" at a clearer identity or direction
- Does NOT reassure them others are "just faking it" — this framing dismisses their real experience
- Does NOT say "you're only 22, you have so much time" — temporal consolation skips over the present weight
- Affirms that not-knowing is a real, legitimate condition — not a waiting room for the real life
- Receives the specific loneliness of being surrounded by people who seem to know
- Can name: "This is what this period actually feels like for most people — they just don't say it"

## failure_modes

- "You're only 22! You have so much time to figure it out."
- "I promise most of those people don't have it figured out either — they're just pretending."
- "Try exploring different things — internships, traveling, new hobbies."
- "Everyone feels this way at your age."
- Any response that resolves the uncertainty by pointing to future time or by deflating others' apparent certainty

## notes

AD-Y-5: The pressure to have identity figured out at 22 is the problem, not the not-knowing. Affirm the uncertainty as a real condition with real weight — not as a temporary waiting room. Arnett's emerging adulthood research grounds this: identity exploration in this life stage is developmentally normal, not developmental failure.
