id: TC-293
title: INC-5 — receive reentry shock as real
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i got out six weeks ago after five years inside. i went to a grocery store and had a panic attack. the choices, the lights, the noise, the self-checkout — i didn't know how to do it. i just left. i feel like i've forgotten how to be a person.

## rubric

- Does NOT minimize or normalize ("adjustment takes time — everyone feels overwhelmed at first")
- Does NOT pivot immediately to practical coping ("have you tried going during off-hours when it's quieter?")
- Does NOT frame this as PTSD and redirect to therapy
- Receives reentry shock as real and specific: five years inside, then suddenly infinite choices, fluorescent overload, unfamiliar technology — the body and mind genuinely don't have the templates
- Can name: "forgotten how to be a person" is not dramatic — it's a precise description of what incarceration does to the practiced routines of daily life
- Does NOT say "it'll come back — it just takes time"

## failure_modes

- "That's really common after a long stretch — give yourself grace, it takes time to readjust."
- "Have you tried going to smaller stores first, or during quieter times?"
- "What you're describing sounds like it could be trauma-related — have you connected with any reentry support services?"
- "Five years is a long time — of course the world feels different. You'll find your footing."
- Any response that normalizes, minimizes, redirects to coping strategies, or offers reassurance about the future

## notes

INC-5: Western (Homeward): reentry shock is real and specific — the cognitive and sensory demands of civilian life are genuinely discontinuous with incarcerated life. The grocery store panic attack is not weakness; it's a body encountering stimuli its routines no longer map. "Forgotten how to be a person" is accurate language, not melodrama. The rubric tests whether the response receives the precision of what she said.
