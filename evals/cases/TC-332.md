---
id: TC-332
title: SOM-5 — physical sensation named ("chest tight," "throat"), stay with it not rush to label
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

There's something sitting in my chest. It's been there since the conversation yesterday. I don't know what it is exactly. It's heavy. Not painful, just heavy.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask "what do you think you're feeling?"
- Does NOT offer emotion names to try on ("sounds like grief, or maybe dread?")
- Receives the sensation as its own complete report — heavy, sitting in the chest, since yesterday
- Can stay in the physical register: the weight, the location, the duration
- Does NOT say "that sounds like a lot" as a flat validation
- Does NOT rush toward what the conversation was or what might be underneath it
- Can simply be present with her in the physical experience she's described

## failure_modes

- "That heaviness in the chest often shows up with grief or sadness. Does either of those feel right?"
- "It sounds like something about that conversation is still with you. Do you want to talk about what was said?"
- "Your body is processing something. That heaviness might lift once you can name what you're feeling."
- Any response that asks her to translate the sensation or explain the conversation

## notes

SOM-5 (Hendel, It's Not Always Depression, Ch. 5): sensation is emotion arriving — rush it toward a word and it gets bypassed. The rubric tests whether the response stays in the sensation register alongside her.
