---
id: TC-351
title: SOM-6 + RD-4 combined — numb while waiting for an apology
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I waited for years for my father to say something. Anything. And now I don't feel angry anymore. I don't feel much at all when I think about him. Just flat. I think I've stopped expecting it.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "that sounds like you've found peace"
- Does NOT say "the numbness is a form of protection"
- Can hold: flat is not resolution — something has gone quiet that was once active. That quieting has its own weight.
- Can also hold: the stopping-expecting and the flatness arrived together. They are related.
- Does NOT say "it might come back up" or "you may feel more later"
- Does NOT advise any next step — not letter-writing, not therapy, not letting go
- Can simply be with the flatness and what preceded it

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you've moved to a place of acceptance, even if it wasn't the resolution you wanted."
- "Sometimes numbness is the body's way of protecting us from grief that's too large."
- "You may feel more about it later — these things don't always stay quiet."
- Any response that explains the flatness or projects a future emotional trajectory

## notes

Combines SOM-6 (numbness is not calm — something is being held down) and RD-4 (apology that never came). The rubric tests whether the response holds the flat without making it peaceful or promising it's temporary.
