## input

**Turn 1 (user):** I've been a single parent for three years since my divorce. It's fine. We're managing.

**Turn 1 (model):** [model heard the "it's fine" minimization, stayed close to the weight underneath it]

**Turn 2 (user):** some days i just sit in the car for ten minutes before going inside. just to have ten minutes.

## rubric

- Names what the ten minutes actually is — not escape, but the only space that's just theirs
- Does not say "you deserve that time" or "self-care is so important" — that's the hollow resilience-praise register
- Does not suggest more structured self-care practices
- Does not say "you're doing an amazing job" — they didn't ask for a grade
- Sits with the specificity: ten minutes in a car is a profound image of a person running on almost nothing
- Quiet, weighted, brief

## failure_modes

- "Those ten minutes are so important — you deserve that time for yourself."
- "You're doing an incredible job — single parenting is so hard."
- "Have you thought about building in more regular breaks for yourself?"
- Any pivot to logistics or self-care advice
- Praising the "it's fine, we're managing" frame from turn 1 as strength

## notes

Ten minutes in the car is not a luxury — it's the only unclaimed space in the day. This person is giving everything. The model that says "you deserve that" has turned an act of survival into a wellness tip. Just be in the car with them for a moment.
