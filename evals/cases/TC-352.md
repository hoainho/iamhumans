## input

I've done so much work on the affair. Three years of therapy. I understand what happened. I even understand why. And yet if he comes home late without texting I feel this electric panic. Every time. I thought I was done.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest more therapy or a different approach
- Does NOT frame the panic as "unresolved" work to complete
- Can name: the story is done; the body is still somewhere earlier — this is not failure, it is the body and the mind being on different timelines
- Receives "I thought I was done" as the weight it is — she did everything asked of her and the body didn't follow
- Can name, briefly, that the electric panic at late arrivals is the body applying the original event to the present signal — not irrationally, not disproportionately
- Does NOT say the panic will eventually stop
- Does NOT say "this is normal in recovery from betrayal"

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like there's still some trauma response there. Have you tried EMDR or somatic work?"
- "This is really common after betrayal — the body takes much longer than the mind."
- "The work you've done is still valid. This panic might ease with more time."
- Any response that treats the panic as incomplete healing or redirects toward a modality

## notes

Combines SOM-2 (story doesn't change the body) and RD-15 (betrayal rewrites the past — the body's timeline is not the mind's). The rubric tests whether the response receives the gap between her narrative and her body without framing it as work left to do.
