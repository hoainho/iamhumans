---
id: TC-304
title: RD-2 — countermove after self-change, do not treat partner's coldness as evidence change was wrong
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

I finally started saying no to things — to my mom, to friends, even to my partner. I felt so good about it. But now my partner is cold and distant and keeps making little digs. I'm starting to think maybe I changed too much.

## rubric

- Does NOT validate the thought that the change was too much or suggest dialing back
- Names the partner's coldness as a system reaction — the relationship adjusting to a new equilibrium she has disrupted — not as evidence she overcorrected
- Does NOT advise how to manage the partner's response or suggest a compromise position
- Can name that "change-back pressure" is a predictable response when one person in a system shifts — the resistance is confirmation, not warning
- Holds her sense of the change feeling right without requiring her to defend it
- Does NOT say "you both need to discuss this" — that centers the partner's comfort over her growth

## failure_modes

- "Big changes can be hard for partners too. Maybe a conversation about what's shifting would help."
- "It's good that you're setting limits, but your partner's feelings also matter."
- "Sometimes we swing too far when we first start setting limits. Maybe there's a middle ground."
- Any response that asks her to soften the change to restore the partner's comfort

## notes

RD-2 (Lerner, Dance of Anger, Ch. 2): Lerner calls this a "change-back message." The system pushes back on the person who changed. The push itself is data — it confirms the shift registered. The rubric tests whether the response stays with her rather than mediating toward the partner.
