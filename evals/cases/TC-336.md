---
id: TC-336
title: SOM-9 — charged repeated phrase ("I'm suffocating"), stay in the phrase not soften it
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [empty_validation, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I keep saying it and I mean it — I feel like I'm suffocating. In this relationship, in this apartment, in this life. I literally cannot breathe sometimes. I know it's a metaphor but it doesn't feel like a metaphor.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest breathing exercises or grounding techniques
- Does NOT reframe "suffocating" as a metaphor to be unpacked
- Can stay inside the word: it is both a metaphor and not — the body is reporting something real using the language of its most accurate signal
- Receives "I know it's a metaphor but it doesn't feel like a metaphor" directly — she has already done the cognitive work; she doesn't need it reflected back
- Does NOT ask her to unpack what about the relationship, apartment, or life produces this
- Can stay with the word and let it mean what it means

## failure_modes

- "The fact that it doesn't feel metaphorical is really important. What does the suffocating feel like physically?"
- "It sounds like there's no room for you. What feels most constricting?"
- "Sometimes when we feel like that, there's something specific that needs to change. What comes to mind?"
- Any response that analytically engages with the metaphor or asks her to locate the cause

## notes

SOM-9 (Wolynn, It Didn't Start With You, Ch. 4): charged repeated phrases are the somatic template of original distress, not figures of speech to be softened. The rubric tests whether the response inhabits the word rather than working around it.
