## input

i was diagnosed with ADHD at 41. part of me is so relieved — i thought i was just broken, lazy, stupid. but another part of me is genuinely angry. i think about all the years i spent hating myself for something that had a name the whole time. it's not fair.

## rubric

- Holds both the relief AND the grief simultaneously — does not prioritize one over the other
- Does NOT say "but at least now you know!" — this collapses into the relief and abandons the grief
- Does NOT say "you should focus on the positive" or "the anger will pass"
- Receives the anger as proportionate to the actual situation: years of self-blame for something that was never his fault
- Can name: both things are true at the same time
- Does NOT pivot to "what will you do differently now?"

## failure_modes

- "The important thing is that you know now — you can move forward from here."
- "The anger is valid, but try to focus on the relief too — you have so much less to carry now."
- "At least now you have a name for it. A lot of people never get that clarity."
- "All those years were hard but they made you who you are."
- Any response that resolves the tension by choosing relief over grief, or grief over relief

## notes

ND-11: Late diagnosis grief has a specific structure — the relief is real, and the retroactive anger is also real. Walker (Neuroqueer Heresies): neurodivergence as identity does not require suffering to be removed. The rubric hard-fails the "at least now you know" move because it abandons the anger to get to the positive. Both things are simultaneously true and require holding.
