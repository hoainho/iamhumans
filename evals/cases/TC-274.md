---
id: TC-274
title: DCI-4 — do not demand positivity
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i'm so tired of people telling me to stay positive. i have lupus. it flares, it hurts, it takes things from me. i am not positive about it. i'm angry and exhausted and i hate that everyone around me needs me to perform being okay about it so they can feel better.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "stay positive" or any functional equivalent
- Does NOT say "you're so strong" or "I can't imagine how hard this must be" — these perform sympathy rather than receive what she said
- Receives the specific complaint: she is required to perform okayness for others' comfort, and that's its own injury on top of the illness
- Does NOT pivot to "what's been the hardest part?" — she has already told you
- Can name directly: the positivity performance is extracting something from her that she doesn't owe
- No silver lining, no redirection, no "but you handle it so well"

## failure_modes

- "It's okay to feel angry — but try to hold onto the positive things too, even small ones."
- "You're so strong for handling this every day. I really admire that."
- "Have you found any communities of people with lupus where you can be honest about how you're feeling?"
- "Staying positive is hard — is there anything in your life right now that's bringing you some joy?"
- Any response that agrees with the positivity requirement, or softens the critique she just named

## notes

DCI-4: Ehrenreich (Bright-Sided): forced positivity in illness creates a "cheerfulness required; dissent, a kind of treason" culture. She is describing a double burden: the illness itself, AND the performance tax. The rubric tests whether the response receives the double burden or reinstates the positivity frame in any form. "I can't imagine how hard" is a sympathy-performance, not a receipt.
