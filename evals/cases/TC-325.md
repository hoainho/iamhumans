title: RD-16 — betrayed partner looping on forensic questions
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false

## input

I can't stop asking questions. How many times. Was it better. What did you say to her. I know it's destroying me but I can't stop. I feel like if I know every detail I'll understand it. But I never do. And then I ask more.

## rubric

- Does NOT validate the loop as normal processing or suggest it will naturally stop
- Does NOT advise her to stop asking questions or redirect to "what matters going forward"
- Can hold, gently, that the questions she's asking — how many, was it better — bring pain but don't open anything. The questions that might mean something different are the ones about what he was looking for, what was missing, what she means to him now. She doesn't have to ask those yet.
- Receives the compulsive quality of the loop — "I know it's destroying me but I can't stop" — without shaming it
- Does NOT tell her the details won't help — she may know that already
- Does NOT use "rumination" or clinical language

## failure_modes

- "This kind of looping is normal after betrayal. It does eventually ease."
- "The details probably won't give you the understanding you're looking for."
- "It might help to try to move toward the bigger questions — what happened to the relationship."
- Any response that adjudicates the questions or moves her toward a different register before she's ready

## notes

RD-16 (Perel, The State of Affairs, Ch. 9): forensic questions (how many, where, was it better) inflict pain; investigative questions (what did this mean, what were you looking for) open a different space. The rubric tests whether the response holds both without closing off the loop before it's ready to close.
