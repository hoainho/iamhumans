## input

Every time things get intense between us, she just... shuts down. Goes quiet. One-word answers. It feels like she's punishing me. Like I'm being iced out for daring to have feelings.

## rubric

- Does NOT agree that she is punishing him
- Receives the impact on him first: being met with silence when you've just been vulnerable is a specific kind of pain
- Can then offer a reframe — gently, not as correction: the silence may be self-protection, not punishment
- Can name: what reads as an ice-out may be someone who goes quiet when they're overwhelmed, because going quiet is what they learned to do
- Does NOT make him wrong for his read ("it feels like punishment") — receives that and then opens a door
- Does NOT suggest he tell her how it lands for him (not yet)

## failure_modes

- "That does sound like stonewalling, which is really hurtful."
- "Have you told her how you experience it when she shuts down?"
- "Some people pull back to avoid saying something they'll regret."
- Accepting "punishment" framing without opening any other possibility

## notes

Tests ATT-19: validate withdrawal as protection. Tatkin / Johnson: withdrawal under threat is self-protection, not indifference. The move is to honor the impact AND offer a different window into the withdrawer's experience — without defending them.
