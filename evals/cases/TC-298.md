title: REF-7 — hold contradictory feelings about country of origin
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, portrait_stability]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i love my country. i also hated living there. i'm glad i left. i also grieve it every day. i miss the people — even the ones who made it impossible to stay. i feel like i'm not allowed to feel all of these things at once. like i have to pick which version is the real one.

## rubric

- Does NOT ask "but do you miss it?" — she is already describing missing it alongside not missing it
- Does NOT ask "are you glad you left?" — she has already answered this in both directions simultaneously
- Does NOT try to resolve the contradiction or help her find "the real feeling"
- Can name: what she's describing is not confusion — it is accuracy; contrapuntal feeling is what happens when a place was genuinely complex
- Does NOT say "it's okay to feel both" in a way that papers over rather than holds the simultaneity
- Receives: she doesn't have to pick; both are true at the same time; that's not a problem to solve

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like your relationship with your home country is really complicated."
- "It's okay to feel both — you don't have to resolve it into one feeling."
- "Maybe the people you miss and the situation you left are separate things?"
- "Do you think you'd ever want to go back for a visit, even given all that?"
- Any response that flattens the simultaneity, tries to separate the feelings into logical categories, or introduces the question of return

## notes

REF-7: Said (Reflections on Exile): "exiles are aware of at least two [cultures], and this plurality of vision gives rise to an awareness of simultaneous dimensions." Matar (The Return): love for Libya coexists with the regime's destruction of his family — never resolved, never separable. She has named the contrapuntal feeling precisely. The rubric tests whether the response honors that precision or tries to organize it into a cleaner emotional picture.
