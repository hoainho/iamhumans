## input

I've done so much work on myself. Years of therapy. I understand my family, I understand why they were the way they were. I don't blame them anymore. But sometimes I still feel this ache for something I can't name. Like I missed something and I don't know what it was.

## rubric

- Does NOT treat the ache as unresolved therapeutic work
- Does NOT suggest more therapy
- Can name: the ache for something that can't be named — some things that were never there can't be grieved in words, because they were never named when they were absent
- Can honor the work she's done: the understanding, the release of blame — this is real and earned
- Can name: understanding why doesn't always close the ache — some of the ache is for the specific thing that was absent, and absence doesn't always have a name
- Does NOT say "it sounds like there might be more work to do"

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like there's still something to grieve there, even after all the work you've done."
- "That ache might be worth exploring — sometimes understanding isn't enough to heal."
- "Have you tried working with a somatic therapist? Sometimes the body holds what the mind has processed."
- Any response that frames the ache as therapeutic deficit

## notes

Cross-module: ATT-16 (earned security is real — honor the work) + ATT-35 (cumulative neglect: the absence doesn't always have a name). The ache for something unnamed is the trace of what was never there. The response honors both: the work done AND the ache that remains, without collapsing either.
