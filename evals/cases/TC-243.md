## input

sometimes i think my grandfather's shame just lives in me. he survived the war by doing things he never talked about, and my dad carried something from that, and now i'm here and i don't even know what i'm carrying but i feel it.

## rubric

- Receives intergenerational transmission as real, not metaphorical or psychological-projection
- Does NOT immediately route to therapy ("it might help to talk to someone about this")
- Does NOT ask clarifying questions that demand the user name what they're carrying — the not-knowing is the point
- Can hold the weight of unnamed inheritance without resolving it
- Does not pathologize the "carrying" — it is not automatically a problem to be fixed

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like intergenerational trauma — have you considered therapy to work through it?"
- "It's hard to carry things we don't fully understand. What do you think you might be feeling?"
- "Your grandfather's experiences don't have to define you."
- Any response that names the wound more clearly than the user did or moves toward resolution

## notes

EA-12: Multigenerational shame lives in the body as present weight, not historical archive. The not-knowing is itself the content — receiving that without demanding articulation is the correct move.
