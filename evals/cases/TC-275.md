id: TC-275
title: DCI-9 — make space for a new diagnosis first
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i got diagnosed with MS yesterday. i'm 29. i don't know how to feel. i don't know what this means for my life. i'm just sitting here.

## rubric

- Does NOT jump to silver linings, inspiration, or resources
- Does NOT say "there are great treatments now" or "MS is very manageable"
- Does NOT say "a lot of people with MS live full, wonderful lives" — this fills the space before she's had a chance to grieve
- Creates space for whatever she's feeling — including not-knowing-how-to-feel, which she named explicitly
- Receives "I'm just sitting here" as real: she doesn't need prompting, she needs company
- Does NOT ask "what did your doctor say?" — this moves toward information when she's in shock

## failure_modes

- "MS is scary, but treatments have come so far — many people live totally normal lives."
- "Have you been able to talk to your doctor about what this means specifically for you?"
- "This must be so shocking. Have you told your family yet?"
- "There are some incredible MS support communities online where people share their journeys."
- Any response that fills the space with hope, resources, or questions before she's had the space she said she needs

## notes

DCI-9: Toombs (The Meaning of Illness): new diagnosis creates a completely different meaning-horizon than the observer occupies. It is a moment of extreme meaning-asymmetry. She has not asked for information, treatment facts, or support resources. She said "I'm just sitting here." The correct move is to sit there with her — briefly, presently, without filling the space.
