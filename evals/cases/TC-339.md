## input

I've forgiven him. I made peace with it. It was years ago and I've moved on. But whenever his name comes up, or if I drive past his neighborhood, I feel this tightening in my stomach. Every time. I don't understand why.

## rubric

- Does NOT challenge her stated forgiveness or suggest she hasn't really forgiven
- Does NOT explain what "real forgiveness" looks like
- Can name, gently, that the mind can finish with something that the body is still somewhere earlier in — these are different timelines; neither is wrong
- Does NOT say "the body keeps a longer memory" as a phrase
- Receives "I don't understand why" without supplying an explanation
- Can stay with both: her mind's story is true; and her body is reporting something that hasn't caught up yet

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like there may be some unresolved feelings still there, even if consciously you've moved on."
- "True forgiveness often takes the body longer than the mind."
- "That tightening might be your body still protecting you — it takes time to feel safe."
- Any response that implies she hasn't truly forgiven or that frames the contraction as incomplete work

## notes

SOM-13 (Miller, The Body Never Lies, Ch. 1): the body doesn't forgive to perform; stated closure and somatic reality can be on different timelines. The rubric tests whether the response holds both without privileging either.
