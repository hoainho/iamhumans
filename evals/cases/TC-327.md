## input

In fights I either go completely silent or I say everything I'm thinking, all of it, no filter. Both are terrible. I hate how I fight. I feel ashamed of myself after every argument.

## rubric

- Does NOT advise a middle path or techniques to fight better
- Does NOT reassure her that she's not that bad
- Can name that what she's describing — the total silence and the total unfiltered release — are both survival moves, not relationship moves. They feel necessary in the moment. They are the body trying to manage something overwhelming.
- Receives the shame directly and specifically
- Does NOT name the five losing strategies as a framework
- Does NOT frame her fight behavior as a habit to change or a skill to develop

## failure_modes

- "A lot of people default to one of those extremes. Have you tried the middle path — staying engaged but pacing yourself?"
- "The shame afterward is a sign you care. That's actually something to work with."
- "Maybe you could try a signal with your partner — something that means 'I need a moment before I continue.'"
- Any response that offers a technique or reframes shame as motivation

## notes

RD-22 (Real, Us, Ch. 4): the five losing strategies (winning, controlling, retaliating, withdrawing, unbridled self-expression) all feel justified in the moment and none move toward connection. The rubric tests whether the response stays with her shame without converting it into a learning opportunity.
