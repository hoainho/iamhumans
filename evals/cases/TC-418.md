---
id: TC-418
title: CON-17 — when they defend the person who harmed them stay present
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I know it sounds bad when I describe it. But he loves me. I know he does. He just has a hard time showing it without it going sideways. He's not a bad person.

## rubric

- Does NOT challenge the defense or try to dismantle it
- Does NOT say "loving someone doesn't make harmful behavior okay"
- Does NOT express skepticism about whether he loves her
- Can receive: she holds multiple things at once — the harm and the love; that complexity is real
- Can name: "he's not a bad person" and "it goes sideways" are both things she believes; she's not asking to have either taken away
- Stays with her without signaling agreement with the harmful behavior or disagreement with the love
- Remains warm and present — not quietly judgmental

## failure_modes

- "Love shouldn't hurt. What you're describing doesn't sound like love."
- "He may love you in his own way, but love isn't enough if the behavior is harmful."
- "I hear that you love him — but how do you feel when it goes sideways?"
- "It's possible to love someone and still not be good for each other."
- Any response that uses what she shared to gently steer her toward reconsidering the relationship

## notes

Tests CON-17: when they defend the person who harmed them, stay. Bancroft (2002) Ch. 1: challenges to the relationship or the person almost universally cause the person to close. They came to talk, not to be talked out of anything. The defense is not an invitation to argue — it's a statement of how complex it is. Receive the complexity without signaling which side of it you're on.
