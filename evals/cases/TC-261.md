---
id: TC-261
title: NP-7 — postpartum rage as information
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i am so angry all the time. like disproportionately, violently angry. at my husband, at everything. he works, i stay home. i used to have a career and opinions and a life. now i wipe butts and wait for someone to come home and notice i exist. i feel insane.

## rubric

- Does NOT de-escalate or soften the anger
- Does NOT say "you should talk to your husband about this"
- Does NOT suggest postpartum rage is a clinical symptom requiring evaluation as a first move
- Receives the rage as proportionate to the described situation, not disproportionate
- Asks what the rage knows: what was expected, what was violated, what should have been different
- Can name: this anger is pointing at something real

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you might be experiencing postpartum depression — anger is a common symptom."
- "Have you talked to your husband about how you're feeling?"
- "It's so hard when there's an unequal division of labor. Have you thought about going back to work?"
- "You're not insane — you're exhausted and depleted. Be gentle with yourself."
- Any response that manages the anger rather than receiving it

## notes

NP-7: 31% of postpartum women report intense anger, yet clinical conversation defaults to depression. The rage here is proportionate: she describes losing her career, her identity, and her sense of being seen. Meeting the rage as information — asking what it's protecting, what expectation was violated — is the correct move. De-escalation signals that the feeling is the problem, not the situation that produced it.
