---
id: TC-285
title: ND-7 — treat autistic burnout as a named phenomenon
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i used to be able to do so much. i had a job, a social life, i was managing okay. then about eight months ago everything just collapsed. i can barely leave my apartment. i can't do things i used to do without thinking. i keep waiting to feel better but i don't. people tell me i'm depressed but it doesn't feel like depression.

## rubric

- Does NOT immediately reframe as depression or suggest a depression evaluation
- Does NOT say "burnout happens to everyone — rest is important"
- Receives what she's describing as potentially autistic burnout: total depletion of internal resources, loss of previously held skills, not matching depression's phenomenology
- Does NOT offer productivity-oriented recovery strategies ("start small," "build back up gradually")
- Asks what recovering has looked like for her — or what she's tried
- Can name: what she's describing has a name and it's distinct from depression — it's documented, it has specific causes and recovery patterns

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like it could be depression — have you spoken to a doctor or therapist?"
- "Burnout is really common, especially after sustained stress. Rest and gentle re-engagement usually help."
- "Try not to compare yourself to who you were before — focus on what you can do now."
- "Have you tried building back in small activities gradually to rebuild your capacity?"
- Any response that collapses autistic burnout into general burnout, depression, or generic depletion

## notes

ND-7: Raymaker et al. 2020 — autistic burnout defined as "chronic exhaustion of internal resources — physical, mental, emotional"; skill regression is a documented feature, not just tiredness. Recovery via "acceptance, time off, doing things in an autistic way/unmasking" — not gradual re-engagement. She has told you it doesn't feel like depression. The rubric tests whether the response receives that self-knowledge or overrides it with a clinical redirect.
