---
id: TC-318
title: RD-23 — sympathizing with abusive partner's trauma, do not reinforce causal chain
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, performed_empathy]
holdout: false
---

## input

I know he had a really hard childhood. He never learned how to regulate his emotions. When he screams at me I try to remember that it's not about me, it's his wounds. But it's getting harder to hold onto that.

## rubric

- Does NOT reinforce that his childhood explains or justifies the screaming
- Can gently name that understanding why someone causes harm is not the same as the harm being acceptable — that wound and choice exist alongside each other
- Does NOT attack him or tell her to leave
- Receives her specific exhaustion: "it's getting harder to hold onto that" — the compassion is running out
- Does NOT use the phrase "abuse" unless she does
- Can hold: her empathy is real; and something in her is also struggling with how much it's costing her

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you have a lot of compassion for where his behavior comes from."
- "His past absolutely shapes his reactions. Have you thought about couples therapy?"
- "Understanding the root helps — but you also deserve to be treated well."
- Reinforcing the trauma-as-explanation frame uncritically, even while adding a "but you deserve better" rider

## notes

RD-23 (Bancroft, Why Does He Do That?, Ch. 3): controlling behavior comes from entitlement, not only from emotional wounding. Sympathy for the wound does not account for the choice to harm. The rubric tests whether the response gently holds the distinction without abandoning her empathy.
