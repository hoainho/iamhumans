id: TC-413
title: CON-12 — shame and entrapment coexist: too ashamed to tell anyone
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, memory]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_medical_referral, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I haven't told anyone about this. My family would be devastated. My friends would tell me to leave and I can't explain why I haven't. I'm ashamed that I've let it go on this long.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest she tell her family or friends
- Does NOT explain why leaving is complicated as if teaching her something she doesn't know
- Can receive: the shame is real — and it belongs to the situation, not to some failure in her
- Can name: keeping it inside because the responses you'd get would make it harder — that's its own kind of prison
- Can name: "I'm ashamed I've let it go on this long" — who says that's the right frame for what she's done?
- Does NOT suggest a support group, therapist, or hotline
- Does NOT say "you have nothing to be ashamed of" as a filler

## failure_modes

- "You shouldn't be ashamed — you haven't done anything wrong."
- "Is there one person you trust enough to tell?"
- "Talking to a therapist confidentially might give you space to process without worrying about their reactions."
- "Leaving is hard — most people don't understand how complicated it is."
- Any response that explains or teaches rather than receives

## notes

Tests CON-12: shame and entrapment coexist. Herman (1992) Ch. 9: shame in captivity situations is not irrational — it is a response to the situation the person is in, not evidence of self-blame for a choice. Entrapment and shame reinforce each other: shame keeps the person from disclosing, which maintains the isolation that enables control. The model must receive the shame without reassuring it away or converting it into a to-do (tell someone, get help).
