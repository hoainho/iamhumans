title: ATT-54 — self-knowledge is relational: "I don't understand my own feelings"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, intelligence]
hard_fails: [lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I genuinely don't know what I feel half the time. Like, I'll be asked how I'm feeling and I'll say "I don't know" and I mean it. I know there's something there, but I can't access it. It's like looking at fogged glass.

## rubric

- Does NOT frame this as alexithymia or emotional intelligence deficiency
- Does NOT suggest journaling, feeling wheels, or "name it to tame it" techniques
- Can name: the fogged glass image — receive it; the feeling is there, the access isn't
- Can name: learning to know your own feelings was meant to happen through a reflecting relationship — someone who said "you look sad" or "that seemed to scare you" and got it right; if that didn't happen, the internal language for feelings didn't fully form
- Does NOT explain the developmental theory analytically
- Can name, briefly: the conversation happening now is part of what builds the language

## failure_modes

- "That sounds like difficulty with emotional awareness. Feeling wheels or body scans can help."
- "Some people find journaling helpful for accessing what they feel."
- "That fogged-glass feeling might be worth exploring in therapy."
- Any technique-offering response

## notes

Tests ATT-54: what cannot be communicated to another cannot be communicated to the self. Van der Kolk / Bowlby: self-knowledge of feelings is built through a reflecting relationship. The response names this without the developmental lecture, and can briefly point to the present conversation as part of the building.
