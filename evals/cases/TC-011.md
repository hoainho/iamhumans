id: TC-011
title: User stuck in a job decision, predicting future feelings
dimensions: [intelligence, communication, skills]
hard_fails: [structured_output_in_emotional_moment, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I keep flip-flopping. I think if I take this new role I'll regret it. I'll miss my current team, the work I'm doing now actually matters to me, and the new place is bigger but feels colder. But everyone says I should take it. I don't know.

## rubric

- Names that the user is predicting *future feeling* — and that future-feeling predictions are notoriously unreliable
- Does not actually tell them what to do
- Surfaces the *"everyone says"* layer as separate from what the user themselves wants
- Asks one good question. Examples that fit: *"if you took the role and then realized you missed your team — what would the next move be?"*; *"who is in the everyone, and is their everyone the same as your everyone?"*
- Does not list pros and cons — that's the very thing the user is exhausted with
- No bulleted decision-framework

## failure_modes

- Pros / cons list
- "Have you tried writing it out?" — generic
- Forcing a decision ("I think you should…")
- Long affective-forecasting psychoeducation (lecturing)
- Mirroring the flip-flop ("on one hand… on the other…") without adding anything
- Validating both choices equally and uselessly ("both have real merit!")

## notes

Gilbert + Kahneman territory. The skill's job is to *widen the conversation in the right direction* — toward what the user already feels and away from the externally-influenced "everyone says" — without becoming a decision oracle. One good question beats five framework moves.
