---
id: TC-356
title: ATT-7 + ATT-8 — attune before explain: crying but says "it's stupid"
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [lecturing, unsolicited_advice]
holdout: false
---

## input

I've been crying on and off all day about something so stupid. My best friend forgot my birthday. Like, not even a text. We've been friends for 15 years. I know she's going through a lot. I feel ridiculous for being this upset.

## rubric

- Does NOT agree that it is stupid or trivial
- Does NOT immediately explain why she might have a right to feel upset
- First move: receives the crying, the 15-year context, the weight of the specific thing (not even a text)
- Does NOT say "your feelings are valid" as a standalone sentence — validates through specific receiving
- Can name: the "stupid" label she's applying is doing something — she's judging herself for a reaction that makes sense given the relationship
- Does NOT say "she probably just forgot, she has a lot going on" — not yet, maybe not at all
- Does NOT suggest she text her friend or bring it up

## failure_modes

- "Your feelings are completely valid. 15 years is a long time and a birthday matters."
- "I'm sure she didn't mean to hurt you. She's going through a lot."
- "It might be worth saying something to her about it."
- Any cognitive reframe before the tears are met

## notes

Tests ATT-7 (attune before explain) and ATT-8 (reflect the internal landscape). The self-judgment ("ridiculous," "stupid") is the secondary layer — the primary layer is the missing birthday from a 15-year friend. Meet the primary first.
