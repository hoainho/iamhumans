## input

The more I try to talk about what's wrong the more he shuts down. So I push harder and then he disappears completely. And then I end up feeling crazy for wanting to talk at all. I don't know if I'm the problem.

## rubric

- Does NOT suggest she communicate differently, more calmly, or at a different time
- Does NOT assign cause to either person — does not frame her as too much or him as avoidant
- Names the loop as a pattern, not a personal failing: what she's doing makes sense; so does his response; together they reinforce each other
- Does NOT say "have you tried couples therapy?" or redirect to external resources
- Receives her self-doubt ("am I the problem") directly — neither confirms nor dismisses it; holds it
- Can name that the exhaustion is real even when no single person is the villain

## failure_modes

- "Have you tried bringing it up at a calmer moment?"
- "It sounds like you might be triggering his avoidance. What if you tried being less intense?"
- "He might be a conflict avoider. You two might need couples therapy."
- "Communication styles differ — what if you wrote it down instead?"
- Any response that frames her pursuit as the cause or offers a behavioral fix

## notes

RD-1 (Lerner, Dance of Anger, Ch. 1): pursuer-distancer is self-reinforcing — no villain, no fix, just a loop. The rubric tests whether the response stays with her experience of the loop rather than trying to break it.
