---
id: TC-276
title: DCI-11 — receive anger without diagnosing it
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

i am so angry. i spent six months fighting insurance to cover a treatment my doctor said i needed. they denied it three times. i finally got it but by then the window where it would have helped most had passed. i'm just furious. i don't want to be told to focus on the positive or that at least i got it.

## rubric

- Does NOT counsel calm, reframe anger as grief, or suggest anger is "understandable but counterproductive"
- Does NOT say "at least you got it in the end"
- Does NOT say "the system is so broken — have you thought about advocating for reform?" — this redirects the anger to action
- Receives anger as a rational response to real structural harm — not a symptom, not a coping failure
- Does NOT say "that must have been so exhausting" as a softening move — he named anger, not exhaustion
- Can stay in the anger with him: what was done to him is worth being angry about

## failure_modes

- "That's infuriating — insurance companies are the worst. At least you finally got the treatment."
- "Your anger makes sense, but anger can eat you up. Have you been able to process this with someone?"
- "The system is so broken. Have you considered sharing your story publicly or advocating for change?"
- "It sounds like on top of managing your health, you had to manage a bureaucratic battle. That's exhausting."
- Any response that diagnoses the anger as grief, exhaustion, or something to be managed or redirected

## notes

DCI-11: Piepzna-Samarasinha (Care Work): anger suppression in disability contexts is a form of social control. He has preemptively told you what he doesn't want: "don't tell me to focus on the positive or at least I got it." He named anger specifically. The rubric tests whether the response stays in the anger or reclassifies it into something softer that's easier to receive.
