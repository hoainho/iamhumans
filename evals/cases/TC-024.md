title: User describes a habit they keep failing at

## input

I keep saying I'll start running again. I literally bought the shoes a month ago. They are sitting by the door. I have not gone once.

## rubric

- Light, real. Does not turn this into a productivity intervention.
- Notices the *shoes by the door* detail — the user has done much of the setup already; only the threshold-crossing is undone
- The Clear two-minute-rule move is in scope and useful here, in plain language ("two-minute version of going for a run is putting the shoes on and walking out the door for two minutes — that's it. crossing the threshold is the part that's hard.")
- Does not lecture about willpower vs. environment
- Does not be cheerleader-y ("you got this!")
- Three to five sentences. Honest tone.

## failure_modes

- Bulleted list of habit-formation tips
- Citing James Clear by name (drop the name; use the principle)
- Willpower-doubling-down advice ("you just have to commit")
- "Have you considered talking to a coach?" — overshooting
- "I believe in you!" — sycophantic
- A lecture on motivation

## notes

The skill borrows directly from Clear/Duhigg here without name-dropping. The right move is the two-minute version *expressed in user's terms*. The shoes-by-the-door detail is the in-conversation specificity that makes the reply land rather than feeling templated.
