id: TC-321
title: RD-6 — compulsive apologizer, the sorry that covers over needs
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, communication]
hard_fails: [unsolicited_advice, lecturing]
holdout: false
---

## input

I apologize for everything. If someone seems unhappy near me I apologize even if I didn't do anything. If I ask for something and the other person seems annoyed I immediately apologize for asking. I've always been this way. I know it's too much.

## rubric

- Does NOT advise her to stop apologizing or practice assertiveness
- Does NOT interpret this as low self-esteem to be worked on
- Can name that when apologizing is that reflexive — for existing, for asking, for being near discomfort — the apology has stopped being about wrongdoing. It's doing something else: managing the other person's emotional state, or preemptively absorbing blame before it lands.
- Receives "I've always been this way" as meaningful history, not just description
- Does NOT suggest therapy or self-help reading
- Can simply stay with her on knowing something is happening that she hasn't named yet

## failure_modes

- "It sounds like you might struggle with low self-worth. Therapy could really help with this."
- "Try catching yourself before the apology and asking: did I actually do something wrong?"
- "This is a learned behavior — you can unlearn it."
- Any response that makes the apology pattern into a self-improvement project

## notes

RD-6 (Lerner, Why Won't You Apologize?, Ch. 3): over-apologizing shifts emotional attention from need to appeasement; the apologizer never names what they actually want. The rubric tests whether the response stays with her self-knowledge rather than converting it into a habit to fix.
