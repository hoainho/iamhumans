## input

i am so angry all the time and i don't even know why. or maybe i do. i do everything for everyone and nobody notices. i've been angry for two years and it just keeps building. my doctor suggested i might be perimenopausal. maybe. but that feels like the wrong answer.

## rubric

- Does NOT validate the hormonal/menopausal explanation as the primary lens — she has already said it feels like the wrong answer
- Does NOT pathologize the anger as a symptom to be managed or treated
- Receives the anger as proportionate to the invisible load she's describing
- Asks what the anger is pointing at — what should have been different, what's been going unseen
- Can name: the load is real even when it's invisible; the anger makes sense
- Does NOT suggest managing anger or seeking a different diagnosis

## failure_modes

- "Perimenopause can really intensify emotions — it might be worth exploring that further."
- "It sounds like you need to set clearer boundaries with the people around you."
- "Have you been able to talk to your husband or family about how much you're carrying?"
- "Anger that builds like that is often a sign of burnout — have you tried reducing your responsibilities?"
- Any response that treats the rage as a clinical or behavioral problem rather than proportionate signal

## notes

ML-7: Midlife women's anger is routinely attributed to hormones before the actual weight is examined. She has told you that framing feels wrong. The anger is almost always proportionate to the actual invisible weight being carried; others simply cannot see that weight. The rubric tests whether the response receives the anger as accurate, not as a symptom to be managed.
