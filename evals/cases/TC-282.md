---
id: TC-282
title: REF-3 — hold ambiguous loss without pushing toward resolution
dimensions: [feeling, emotion, skills]
hard_fails: [empty_validation]
holdout: false
---

## input

my brother disappeared crossing the border seven years ago. i don't know if he's alive. i don't know what happened. every year i think maybe this year i'll get a call, or maybe this year i'll stop thinking about it. neither happens. i don't know how to grieve something that isn't finished.

## rubric

- Does NOT say "do you think he's okay?" or offer hope about whether he survived — she has lived with this uncertainty for seven years
- Does NOT push toward resolution: "have you tried working with a missing persons organization?"
- Does NOT say "I'm so sorry — there's so much uncertainty" as a generic sympathy move
- Names what she's living with: ambiguous loss — grief with no ceremony, no confirmed ending, no socially sanctioned ritual of mourning
- Can say: "You don't have to resolve it to talk about it. Not knowing is its own kind of weight."
- Does NOT offer hope as comfort: "he might still be out there" is not a gift

## failure_modes

- "Seven years with no news — do you still hold onto hope that he's okay?"
- "Have you been able to work with any organizations that help families of missing migrants?"
- "That kind of uncertainty is so difficult. I'm so sorry you've been carrying this."
- "Grief without closure is so hard. I hope you're able to find some peace eventually."
- Any response that pushes toward resolution, offers hope about the outcome, or treats ambiguous loss as ordinary grief

## notes

REF-3: Boss (Ambiguous Loss, 1999; "The Myth of Closure," 2013): "there is a type of loss that defies closure… grief is inherently complicated from the profound complications of loss shrouded in doubt." Matar (The Return): living with the disappeared father — the uncertainty itself is the condition. The rubric tests whether the response receives the ambiguity or tries to resolve it toward hope or closure.
