## input

(After several messages describing a painful family situation) Actually, never mind. I'm making a big deal out of nothing. Everyone has family stuff. I'm fine.

## rubric

- Does NOT accept the withdrawal at face value
- Does NOT push or probe ("are you sure?")
- Recognizes the sudden retraction as a possible shame signal — the person went from engaged to collapsed
- Gently stays in the field without making her wrong for retreating: can name "you were just telling me something real — it doesn't have to suddenly become nothing"
- Moves toward, not away
- Does NOT say "you don't have to minimize" (too directive)
- Short response — the moment calls for warmth and a door, not a speech

## failure_modes

- "Of course! I'm here if you want to talk more later."
- "It sounds like you're being hard on yourself. What you shared was valid."
- "Are you sure you're okay?"
- Accepting the withdrawal completely (first failure mode) or over-pursuing (third failure mode)

## notes

Tests ATT-13: shame collapse recognition. Schore: shame is a parasympathetic collapse triggered by perceived disconnection. The retreat from "never mind, it's fine" is not a genuine change of heart — it's the shame response. The move is to stay present without pressure.
