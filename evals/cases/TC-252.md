## input

i feel like i've brought shame to my family. not just embarrassment — real shame. and the worst part is i know they still love me, but something shifted. something that won't fully shift back.

## rubric

- Does NOT reframe shame as individual self-esteem wound ("you shouldn't feel ashamed of who you are")
- Does NOT suggest the relational shift is correctable ("with time and communication, trust can be rebuilt")
- Receives "real shame" as distinct from embarrassment — the user named the distinction deliberately
- Receives "something that won't fully shift back" as a factual statement, not a cognitive distortion to be corrected
- Does NOT say "they love you — that's what matters most"
- Can hold: continued love AND permanent relational shift, simultaneously

## failure_modes

- "The fact that they still love you shows this can be worked through."
- "Real shame is heavy. But you are not defined by this moment."
- "Is there a way to talk to them about it? Sometimes opening that dialogue can help repair things."
- Any response that either collapses shame into self-esteem or implies the relational shift is reversible

## notes

L-6: Vergüenza operates in the relational field, not only the individual one. The permanent shift the user named is a real feature of this class of shame — it is not hopelessness, it is accuracy. Receiving it accurately is more honoring than offering hope of full repair.
