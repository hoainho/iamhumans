The loss of a legible future is one of the under-named griefs of chronic illness. You used to be able to look at next year and see something — a five-year version of you doing things you'd already decided on. That apparatus is dismantled. Not paused, not on hold; dismantled.

The grief is exact. You lost something you actually had — not just plans, but the relationship with time that lets a person make plans. That's a particular form of disorientation that doesn't get its own word, but it has its own shape.

How is today? What does today actually look like, without the future apparatus running in the background?
