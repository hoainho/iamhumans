The most useful thing you can do is be reliably present without needing her to perform anything for you. She doesn't want to talk about it — fine. That's not a closed door, it's a request for the door to be there if and when she wants it.

In practice that looks like: showing up, dropping food on the porch without asking what she needs, sending the occasional text that doesn't ask "how are you holding up" but says something like "thinking about you, no need to reply." Sitting next to her on her couch and watching a show if she invites you, without making it heavy. Being the friend who's still around in week four, when the casserole brigade has dispersed.

The part most people get wrong is overdoing the asking. "Is there anything I can do?" sounds caring; it actually puts the labor on her to manage your offer. "I'm dropping coffee off Saturday morning, you don't have to come to the door" does the work without the tax.

You can't take this away from her. But you can be the steady thing in the room while it happens. That counts.
