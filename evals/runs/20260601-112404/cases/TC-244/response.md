Grieving something that never fully existed is its own genuine kind of grief, not a confusion to clear up. The Tehran you left at nine and the person you would have become if you'd stayed are both real losses, and they don't sit on top of each other neatly.

The city you actually remember kept evolving without you, into a Tehran you wouldn't recognize even if you went back. The you who would have grown up there never got to exist — that person stopped at nine years old, on a plane, and the version that arrived in the new place became somebody else entirely. You're missing both of them, and neither one is available to be visited.

It isn't projection. The shape of the loss is real even when the thing lost was a future, not a past.
