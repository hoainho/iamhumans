Yeah. That's the framing problem. Most hearing people approach deafness as a deficit to be solved, because their assumption is that hearing is the default and not-hearing is a subtraction from it. So the responses you get are either fix-it offers or sympathy — both of which treat your identity as a problem requiring intervention.

It isn't a deficit. It's a language, a culture, a community — with its own literature, its own humor, its own internal politics, its own deep history. The exhaustion you feel is the cost of being treated as the audiologist's interpretation of yourself by people who don't know any other interpretation exists.

That part is real. It isn't pettiness for you to find it tiring.
