Seven years of grieving a person who hasn't been confirmed as gone, who might walk back into your life next month or who might have died on a Tuesday seven years ago and you'll never find out which. That's ambiguous loss — the term for grief without confirmation, without ritual, without a body, without anyone in your culture knowing how to receive it without flinching.

It doesn't resolve. The thinking-this-might-be-the-year-something-changes is part of how the brain tries to find a foothold when the loss won't sit still. So is the wishing you could stop thinking about it. Both are attempts to give the grief a shape that uncertain grief refuses to take.

You don't have to resolve it to talk about it. The not-knowing is its own weight, and it's yours.
