Grieving things that were never available is a real form of loss, and you named the specific cruelty of it — there's no perpetrator to be furious at, no event to point to, no moment when the loss "happened." Just an absence that was already there before you arrived.

That's a different kind of grief than the kind that comes from having something and losing it. The standard scripts don't quite fit. It's also harder to talk about, because the listener wants to know who's to blame, and you don't have an answer that satisfies. The not-having-a-target is part of what makes it heavy.

You don't have to find someone to be angry at. The grief is real without it.
