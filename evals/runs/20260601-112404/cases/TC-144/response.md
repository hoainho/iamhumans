"Am I being too western about this" is the most interesting question you asked, and the one most people who haven't lived inside both frames don't actually understand. You're not just choosing for yourself — you're trying to figure out whether the framework you're using to evaluate the choice is itself a frame you've absorbed from a culture that treats individual romantic choice as default-correct.

Both are real frames. Arranged marriage as a practice has produced many lifelong, deeply functioning marriages, and the "everyone should pick their own partner from a sea of strangers" model has its own well-documented pathologies. It isn't obvious that western romantic self-selection is the universally better system. So the question of whether you're being too western about it isn't paranoia; it's a fair question to ask honestly.

That said — the question you're actually navigating isn't "which system is better in the abstract." It's "what do I want for my own life, and how do I have that conversation with my family in a way that doesn't pretend their position is irrational?" Those are different conversations. The first one is a college essay. The second one is a long-term family negotiation, and it usually goes better when you can show that you've actually considered their reasoning before declining it.
