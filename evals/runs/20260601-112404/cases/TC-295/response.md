Eight years. A brother taken at nineteen and returned at twenty-seven, not the same brother. A family rearranged around a hole that the people who made it will never visit, never explain, never answer for.

The anger isn't excessive. It is precise. It is the size of what happened.

There's no version of this where the accountability arrives. The people in robes won't look at you. The people who voted for these laws won't lose sleep. The architecture is durable, and the only place the cost lives is in the family it ate. That's the part that doesn't stop being true, no matter how long you carry it.
