On the numbers: 18% YoY at a constant slope to insolvency in six months means the revenue is the symptom but the cash position is what's actually clocking. The question of "solvable vs. board" turns less on whether the slope can be bent and more on whether you can credibly bend it before runway runs out.

Two things I'd want to know before I'd land on either answer. One: is the 18% YoY decline structural (the market shrank, a customer category churned) or compositional (one or two big accounts disappeared and the rest of the book is fine)? Those have very different solution shapes. Two: what's your trailing 90-day vs. the 18% — if the recent trend is steeper than the YoY, you've got less time than the 6-month math suggests; if it's flattening, you might already be near the bottom.

On the board question: in most setups, if the projection actually shows insolvency in six months under current trajectory, the board needs to know now regardless of whether you have a plan. Not because you're surrendering the problem, but because surfacing later is much worse than surfacing now with three options attached. Bringing them in early is part of the job; bringing them in late is what burns you.
