"Meeting a stranger" — that captures it. The 22-year-old in those journals isn't just a younger version of you. She's someone you've moved far enough from that her interior life looks like another person's.

You don't have to land on sad-for-her or proud-of-now. Those are two stories the brain offers because closure is more comfortable than holding both at once. The honest version is that both are real, and they don't resolve cleanly. She was in pain you didn't fully understand at the time. You've come a long way she couldn't have imagined was even possible. Neither of those facts subtracts from the other.

The right thing to do with the journals isn't usually a feeling — it's just to sit with the fact that the distance between then and now is real and earned.
