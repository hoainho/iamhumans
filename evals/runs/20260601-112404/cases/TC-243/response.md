The unnamed thing is real, and it doesn't need to be named to be doing what it's doing.

Some inheritances skip the explicit conversation. A man who survives by doing things he won't speak of leaves a particular kind of silence in the house his children grow up in. That silence shapes a son who carries something he can't quite point to, and that son shapes the room his child grows up in, and eventually you're sitting here knowing the weight is yours and not knowing what's in it. The not-knowing is part of the inheritance — the original shame is in there because it was kept silent, and the silence got handed down with it.

You don't have to figure out what you're carrying for it to be true that you're carrying it. The acknowledgment that it's real is its own small piece of work.
