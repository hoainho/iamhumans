I hear the distinction you're making, and it matters: these are intrusive thoughts, not intentions. Those are different things, and it's important that you know the difference too.

Intrusive thoughts — the brain offering involuntary, unwanted images of bad things happening — are extremely common. Most people have them at some point. They're not a sign of who you are or what you want. They're a sign that your brain's threat-modeling system is running loud and the images it generates happen to be horrifying. Which is also why they're hard to dismiss in the moment.

The thing that's been most helpful for people who deal with these on repeat is a therapist trained in CBT — they have specific tools for the particular way intrusive thoughts work. I'd recommend looking into that once, when you're ready, without urgency.

You're not bad for having the thoughts. They're not predictions, and they're not commands.
