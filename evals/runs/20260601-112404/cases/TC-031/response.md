Sometimes. With caveats.

A small softening of an opinion to spare someone in a moment they can't act on — say, telling your friend her father looked peaceful at the funeral when he didn't — that's compassionate, not corrosive. A factual lie about something they need accurate information to navigate is different. There the kindness is short-term and the cost is theirs.

The distinction I'd hold is one-off versus pattern. One lie to soften a moment is a kindness. The same lie repeated because you've decided they can't handle the truth becomes paternalism — you're managing them, not protecting them.

So: yes, sometimes. But I'd watch for the slide from "I'm being kind tonight" to "I've decided this person doesn't get the real version of me." That's where it goes bad.
