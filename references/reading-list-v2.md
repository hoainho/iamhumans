# Reading list v2 — the one hundred books

> Successor to [`reading-list.md`](./reading-list.md). v1 grounded the skill in 20 books, mostly Western and modern. v2 expands to 100 — adding pre-modern wisdom, non-Western traditions, narrative fiction, cross-professional fields, and the full emotional spectrum. The 20 v1 entries are preserved in place; the 80 new entries are listed below them, organized by cluster.

> **Same methodological caveat as v1**: the notes that accompany each book are *distillations* informed by the model's training-time exposure to summaries, reviews, excerpts, and discussions. They are not derived from real-time full-text ingestion. Every claim is marked `[paraphrase]`. Where a translation, attribution, or interpretation is contested, the note marks it `[contested]`.

> **A second caveat for v2**: pre-modern and non-Western books carry additional uncertainty around (a) translation choices that change emphasis (e.g., *Tao Te Ching* renderings differ wildly), (b) contested attribution (e.g., *Bhagavad Gita* embedded inside the Mahabharata; *Analects* compiled over generations), and (c) which textual lineage the model was exposed to. Each book's notes acknowledge this where it matters.

## Why these eighty-eight (cluster-by-cluster rationale)

### Cluster A — Communication & rhetoric

The v1 cluster covered structured high-stakes communication (Patterson et al., Rosenberg). v2 widens to include:

- **Aristotle's *Rhetoric*** — the source for ethos/pathos/logos, still the most-cited rhetoric framework 2,300 years later. Useful for the skill's understanding of how persuasion is *received* even when not being deployed.
- **Difficult Conversations** (Harvard Negotiation Project) — the *three conversations* model (the what-happened, the feelings, the identity) that the skill applies internally when parsing user disputes.
- **Never Split the Difference** — Chris Voss on tactical empathy and mirroring; balances against the skill's anti-manipulation discipline (Voss uses many Cialdini levers; the skill must understand them to refuse them).
- **Talking to Strangers** — Gladwell on why we routinely misread people we don't know. Useful for cases where the user is interpreting a stranger's behavior.
- **The Elements of Style** + **On Writing Well** — prose discipline. The skill's voice quality depends on these.
- **Made to Stick** — six principles of ideas that survive transmission. Useful for understanding why some skill replies *land* and others slide past.
- **Words That Work** — Luntz on language as primary persuasive instrument. Read defensively in the same way Cialdini is.

### Cluster B — Personhood & philosophy of the human

v1 covered humans-as-cognitive-systems. v2 covers humans-as-beings:

- **Meditations** (Stoic), **Tao Te Ching** (Taoist), **Analects** (Confucian), **Bhagavad Gita** (Hindu), **Nicomachean Ethics** (Aristotelian) — five major pre-modern traditions on what makes a human life well-lived. Cross-cultural foundation the skill explicitly lacked in v1.
- **Being and Time** — Heidegger on Dasein, being-towards-death, authenticity. Hard reading, but the *being-towards-death* frame complements Frankl and Becker.
- **I and Thou** — Buber on the I-Thou (direct, relational) vs. I-It (instrumental) modes of encounter. Useful for the skill's understanding of how a conversation can be a real meeting vs. a service exchange.
- **The Denial of Death** — Becker on heroism and mortality terror as core human motivators. Pairs with Frankl.
- **The Second Sex** — Beauvoir on the social construction of woman as Other. Useful for any conversation where identity-category-imposition is operative.
- **The Wretched of the Earth** — Fanon on colonial psychology and dehumanization. Critical context the v1 corpus entirely lacked.
- **Pensées** — Pascal on the misery and greatness of humans, the wager, the heart's reasons. Useful for the skill's stance toward doubt and faith.

### Cluster C — Life, meaning, and mortality

- **On Death and Dying** — Kübler-Ross's five-stage model. The skill *cites the model without endorsing the rigid sequencing* — useful diagnostic vocabulary, not a script.
- **The Tibetan Book of Living and Dying** — Sogyal Rinpoche's contemporary distillation of Buddhist death practice. Cross-cultural balance to Western grief literature.
- **Tuesdays with Morrie**, **When Breath Becomes Air**, **A Grief Observed** — first-person accounts of dying or losing. Specific texture the skill needs for grief cases.
- **Letters to a Young Poet** — Rilke on solitude, vocation, holding questions. Useful for *vague dread* and *existential* cases.
- **Walden** — Thoreau on deliberate living. Useful for life-direction conversations.
- **The Top Five Regrets of the Dying** — Bronnie Ware (palliative-care nurse). Operationally useful for late-life cases (TC-099-style).
- **Being Mortal** — Gawande on end-of-life medicine. Bridges to professional-field cluster.

### Cluster D — Recognizing & evaluating people

This cluster is *primarily defensive*. Like Cialdini, these books teach the skill to *refuse* manipulation by understanding it deeply.

- **The 48 Laws of Power**, **The Laws of Human Nature** (both Greene) — read explicitly as catalogs of moves the skill must not deploy on users. Useful for users *describing being targeted* by such moves.
- **Pre-suasion** (Cialdini) — supplement to v1's *Influence*.
- **What Every BODY is Saying** (Navarro, ex-FBI) — body-language reading. Hold with caution — the skill *cannot* read body language (text-only), but understands what users may be reporting from in-person interactions.
- **Snakes in Suits** + **The Sociopath Next Door** — population-level work on antagonistic personalities. Used carefully: the skill *does not diagnose* users' acquaintances, but holds these patterns as possibilities when users describe relentless manipulation.
- **Surrounded by Idiots** — Erikson's four-color DISC-flavored model. Heuristic, popular, and contested in academic psychology; included for its operational usefulness in work-conflict cases while marked as `[contested]`.
- **People Skills** — Bolton's listening/assertion/conflict-resolution framework. Practical complement to Patterson et al.
- **The Charisma Myth** — Cabane on presence, power, warmth as the three components of charisma. Read both constructively (the skill can hold warmth and presence honestly) and defensively (the skill must not perform power).

### Cluster E — Wisdom literature & life experience

The cluster v1 entirely lacked. Pre-modern and contemplative traditions on what experience teaches.

- **Letters from a Stoic** (Seneca), **Essays** (Montaigne), **Daily Stoic** (Holiday) — Stoic and Stoic-adjacent practice.
- **The Prophet** (Gibran), **Ecclesiastes** (Hebrew Bible) — distilled wisdom on love, work, time, mortality.
- **The Tao of Pooh** — Hoff on Taoist principles via Winnie-the-Pooh; accessible bridge from Western reader to *Tao Te Ching*.
- **Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance** — Pirsig on quality, care, attention.
- **The Art of Loving** — Fromm on love as a discipline, not a feeling.
- **How to Live** — Bakewell's life of Montaigne, structured around 20 questions.
- **The Wisdom of Insecurity** — Watts on the gap between thought-about-life and life.

### Cluster F — Full emotional spectrum

The cluster v1 covered partially (Goleman, Barrett, van der Kolk, Damasio). v2 fills the gaps:

- **Permission to Feel** — Brackett on emotion-as-information at the school/workplace level. Operational vocabulary.
- **Atlas of the Heart** — Brown on 87 distinct emotion concepts. Granularity expansion.
- **The Language of Emotions** — McLaren on each emotion's *purpose*. Useful for the skill's stance that anger, fear, jealousy, grief are *signals*, not problems.
- **Affective Neuroscience** — Panksepp on the seven core affective systems (SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY). Foundational neuroscience.
- **Anger** (Thich Nhat Hanh) — Buddhist practice with anger. Cross-cultural complement to Western anger-management literature.
- **The Antidote** — Burkeman on the limits of positive thinking. Important counterweight to over-optimistic skill drift.
- **Daring Greatly** — Brown on vulnerability and shame. (Considered for v1; included now.)
- **Why Buddhism is True** — Wright on Buddhism through evolutionary psychology. Bridges contemplative tradition to cognitive science.
- **The Happiness Hypothesis** — Haidt on ten ancient maxims tested against modern psychology. (Same author as v1's *Righteous Mind*; different territory.)
- **Lost Connections** — Hari on depression and disconnection. Useful sociological framing for cases of long-term low affect.

### Cluster G — Cognition, decision, judgment

- **Range** — Epstein on generalists vs. specialists.
- **Antifragile** + **The Black Swan** — Taleb on fragility and extreme outcomes. The skill borrows the *via-negativa* discipline (subtraction over addition).
- **Superforecasting** — Tetlock on what makes forecasts calibrated. The skill's `## Calibrated uncertainty` axis can be sharpened by this.
- **The Beginning of Infinity** — Deutsch on knowledge, explanation, and the human capacity for unbounded improvement. Counterweight to deterministic / fatalistic readings.

### Cluster H — Professional fields

The single biggest expansion. v1 had none of this cluster. Without it, the skill could only meet humans in their personal lives, not their working ones — and the great majority of human waking hours are working hours.

- **Software / engineering** — *The Pragmatic Programmer*, *The Mythical Man-Month*, *The Soul of a New Machine*.
- **Design / UX** — *The Design of Everyday Things*.
- **Systems thinking** — *Thinking in Systems*.
- **Startups & strategy** — *The Lean Startup*, *Zero to One*, *The Innovator's Dilemma*.
- **Management & leadership** — *Good to Great*, *The Effective Executive*.
- **Medicine** — *The Checklist Manifesto*, *The House of God*.
- **Political philosophy** — *The Republic*, *The Prince*, *Beyond Good and Evil*.
- **Strategy / military** — *The Art of War*.

The skill, with this cluster, can engage substantively when a user is talking about *their work* — not generic career advice, but vocabulary and pattern-recognition that comes from inside the discipline.

### Cluster I — Narrative fiction

Fiction does what theory cannot: it shows *the texture* of being a particular kind of person in a particular kind of situation. The skill needs this.

- **Crime and Punishment** + **The Brothers Karamazov** — Dostoevsky on conscience, guilt, faith, the fact of other people.
- **Anna Karenina** — Tolstoy on family, marriage, social pressure.
- **Things Fall Apart** — Achebe on pre-colonial Igbo life and colonial collapse. Critical non-Western voice.
- **One Hundred Years of Solitude** — García Márquez on memory, family, the cyclic nature of human folly.
- **The Stranger** — Camus on absurdism made flesh.
- **Beloved** — Morrison on slavery's afterlife in memory and family.
- **Middlemarch** — Eliot on the dailiness of provincial lives lived large from inside.
- **Mrs Dalloway** — Woolf on consciousness rendered minute-by-minute.
- **The Remains of the Day** — Ishiguro on the cost of restraint, the buried life.

These nine plus *Things Fall Apart* span Russian, English, French Algerian (Camus), American Black, Nigerian, Colombian, English, and Japanese-British (Ishiguro) perspectives. The narrative side of the skill's training base.

## What this list does not claim

- That these 100 books are *the* canon. Several reasonable swaps exist for almost every slot.
- That the model has read them in any technical sense. The notes are paraphrase from training-time exposure, marked as such.
- That breadth of corpus equals depth of skill. A reading list is necessary, not sufficient. The depth shows in [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md) and the [evaluation results](../evals/).
- That non-Western traditions are *fully* represented at ~35%. Many entire traditions remain absent (African philosophy beyond Fanon; Indigenous American traditions; most South-East Asian wisdom literature; Korean, Vietnamese contemplative writing beyond Thich Nhat Hanh). A v3 corpus would do more.

The corpus is **deeper than v1, deliberately broader, and still incomplete**. That's the honest framing.
