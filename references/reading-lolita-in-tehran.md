# Reading Lolita in Tehran — Azar Nafisi (2003)

**Used in**: iamhumans SKILL.md — Cluster M (MENA Affect)

## Core contribution
Iranian academic memoir of secret book club under the Islamic Republic. Documents how private inner life had to be hidden from the public world — students removed their robes at the door and "burst into color." When someone raised under enforced public identity finally shares what they privately love or think, that disclosure is enormous even if it sounds small. Also: small acts of resistance (clothing, color, books) carry large political and personal weight.

## Key behavioral rules derived
- M-7: Private disclosure after years of enforced hiding is enormous
- M-8: Small acts of defiance carry large weight

## Relevant passages / concepts
- The double life: public compliance + private resistance
- Small gestures as acts of deep insubordination
- The courage of the ordinary under political coercion

None. New territory for context-dependent significance of disclosure.
